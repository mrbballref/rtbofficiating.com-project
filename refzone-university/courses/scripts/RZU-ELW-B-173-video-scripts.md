## Comprehensive FIBA and EuroLeague Women competition-regulations examination

**Estimated duration:** 10:45  
**Target audience:** Developing EuroLeague Women’s officials  
**Rule/source map:** 2025-26 FIBA Europe FIBA mechanics and game-official guidance — adapted rules, casebook, guidelines, and protocols; 2026-27 EuroLeague Women Regulations — technical rules, competition regulations, disciplinary code, and IRS appendices

### Scene 1 — 00:00-01:20
**Learning purpose:** Frame the authority, effective date, and learning question  
**Location:** RefZone University digital classroom and source-control desk  
**Camera:** Slow push-in to source timeline and course map  
**Action:** The screen identifies Comprehensive FIBA and EuroLeague Women competition-regulations examination, the applicable event type, and the difference between current-effective, future-effective, and event-specific authority.  
**Narration:** This lesson examines Comprehensive FIBA and EuroLeague Women competition-regulations examination. Begin by identifying the EuroLeague season, controlling bylaws, adapted rules, authorized FIBA mechanics guidance, technical and assignment clarification, and assignment directive. FIBA supplies the underlying playing code, but EuroLeague Women competition regulations and authorized event modifications and competition procedures control when they differ. Never apply a future-effective FIBA change or a domestic-league procedure without confirmed EuroLeague adoption.  
**Dialogue:** Instructor: “Name the controlling event document before you analyze the play.”  
**On-screen text:** Competition → Source → Effective date → Ruling  
**Overlays:** FIBA Europe assignment type, Current-effective source, Future-effective source, Human-review gate  
**Decision pause:** Which competition is being officiated? | Which rule code is expressly adopted? | Is the source current effective or future effective?

### Scene 3 — 03:00-05:10
**Learning purpose:** Compare legal, illegal, borderline, and disciplined no-action outcomes  
**Location:** Same court with four original play variations  
**Camera:** Four-panel normal-speed comparison with no slow motion initially  
**Action:** Four original examples isolate one decisive fact for Comprehensive FIBA and EuroLeague Women competition-regulations examination: a legal outcome, an illegal outcome, a borderline view, and a correct no-call or no-correction.  
**Narration:** Compare the plays at normal speed. Do not react only to the final result. For Comprehensive FIBA and EuroLeague Women competition-regulations examination, locate the first relevant action, identify legal status before it, and decide whether the evidence completes every required element.
