## Respect for the Game standards

- Ruleset: 2025-26 NBA Official Playing Rules plus 2025-26 NBA G League Experimental Playing Rules; 2026-27 modifications pending official publication
- Citation map: Applicable NBA Rule 1-14 provision, current G League modification, replay/challenge rule, and assignment directive
- Duration: 10:45

### Scene 1 · 00:00-01:20
**Learning purpose:** Frame the authority, effective date, and learning question  
**Location/camera:** RefZone University digital classroom and source-control desk · Slow push-in to source timeline and course map  
**Action:** The screen identifies Respect for the Game standards, the applicable event type, and the difference between current-effective, future-effective, and event-specific authority.  
**Narration:** This lesson examines Respect for the Game standards. Begin by identifying the NBA G League season, controlling internal regulations, Official Basketball Rules and authorized competition regulations, officiating manual, official interpretation or technical clarification, and assignment directive. NBA G League supplies the underlying playing code, but NBA G League implementations and competition procedures control when they differ. Never apply a future-effective NBA G League change or a domestic-league procedure without confirmed NBA G League adoption.  
**On-screen text:** Competition → Source → Effective date → Ruling  
**Dialogue:** Instructor: “Name the controlling event document before you analyze the play.”  
**Decision pause:** Which competition is being officiated?; Which rule code is expressly adopted?; Is the source current effective or future effective?

### Scene 2 · 01:20-03:00
**Learning purpose:** Teach the governing concept in original RefZone language  
**Location/camera:** Unbranded regulation basketball court and digital tactics board · Broadcast sideline moving to overhead tactical view  
**Action:** An original staged sequence illustrates the legal status and governing conditions for Respect for the Game standards.  
**Narration:** The governing concept is not memorized as isolated wording. For Respect for the Game standards, identify the defined status, location, timing, player responsibility, official responsibility, exception, penalty, and restart. The goal is to recognize the complete condition set in real time.  
**On-screen text:** Status · Location · Timing · Responsibility · Exception  
**Dialogue:** Crew chief: “We are applying the published condition, not a familiar habit from another code.”  
**Decision pause:** What facts must exist before the rule applies?; Which exception could change the result?  
**Ruling reveal:**   

### Scene 3 · 03:00-05:10
**Learning purpose:** Compare legal, illegal, borderline, and disciplined no-action outcomes  
**Location/camera:** Same court with four original play variations · Four-panel normal-speed comparison with no slow motion initially  
**Action:** Four original examples isolate one decisive fact for Respect for the Game standards: a legal outcome, an illegal outcome, a borderline view, and a correct no-call or no-correction.  
**Narration:** Compare the plays at normal speed. Do not react only to the final result. For Respect for the Game standards, locate the first relevant action, identify legal status before it, and decide whether the evidence completes every required element. A dramatic result does not replace rule evidence.  
**On-screen text:** See the first relevant fact  
**Dialogue:** Partner: “My angle does not complete the missing element. Stay with the ruling unless authorized evidence changes it.”  
**Decision pause:** What single fact separates the examples?; Is the available angle complete enough to rule?  
**Ruling reveal:** The ruling follows the complete legal-status and evidence sequence, not appearance, reputation, crowd reaction, or expected outcome.  

### Scene 4 · 05:10-07:30
**Learning purpose:** Apply mechanics, coverage, and administration  
**Location/camera:** Court, scorer’s table, and replay station when applicable · Primary POV, secondary POV, overhead diagram, freeze at status, freeze at decisive fact  
**Action:** The play runs at normal speed, freezes only to verify facts, returns to normal speed, and completes reporting and resumption for Respect for the Game standards.  
**Narration:** Positioning is part of the decision. For Respect for the Game standards, establish the matchup, maintain an open look, observe the complete action, and avoid reaching from a secondary position. After the whistle or administrative decision, confirm player, ball, clock, shot-clock, team-foul, possession, replay, and resumption status.  
**On-screen text:** Angle → Evidence → Classification → Administration  
**Dialogue:** Calling official: “I have the complete action. Confirm the clock and restart.”  
**Decision pause:** Who owns the play?; What help is factual and permitted?; What is the complete administration?  
**Ruling reveal:** Use the authorized signal, report accurately, restore every required game condition, and resume without unnecessary delay.  

### Scene 5 · 07:30-09:10
**Learning purpose:** Demonstrate professional communication and leadership  
**Location/camera:** Court, coach communication lane, crew conference, and postgame reporting room · Table-facing medium shot, coach-side angle, and crew huddle close-up  
**Action:** The crew communicates the decision for Respect for the Game standards briefly, neutrally, and with source-based accuracy.  
**Narration:** Communication does not replace accuracy, but it protects game flow and trust. State only the information needed for Respect for the Game standards. Do not import terminology from another ruleset, debate indefinitely, criticize a partner, or promise an outcome.  
**On-screen text:** Information, not argument  
**Dialogue:** Official: “The ruling is based on the event’s controlling rule and the observed status. We will resume at the designated location with the corrected clock.”  
**Decision pause:** Who should communicate?; What does the coach need to know now?; What must be documented later?

**Action:** The learner answers the decision pause, explains Respect for the Game standards aloud, saves a note, and defines a measurable next-assignment behavior.

Cite the controlling source, explain the decision in original language, administer the entire result, and record one behavior to apply on the next assignment.
