# RZU-NFHS-M 602 Video Production Scripts

**Course:** Crew-Chief Leadership and Crew Performance  
**Program:** NFHS Basketball Officiating Master’s-Level Advanced Officiating Program  
**Rules season:** 2026–27  
**Status:** Complete graduate production draft — qualified rules, mechanics, copyright, accessibility, assessment, video, and program review required  

All narration, scenarios, dialogue, overlays, assessments, diagrams, and production instructions are original RefZone University material. Official publications are reference authorities and are not reproduced.

## Crew Chief as Facilitator, Not Dominator

**Estimated duration:** 14–16 minutes plus embedded decision pause  
**Rule references:** 2026–27 NFHS Basketball Rules Book — current effective licensed reference; Current authorized NFHS Basketball Case Book and official interpretations; 2025–27 NFHS Basketball Officials Manual; NFHS Basketball Rules 2, 3, and 10; Current officials manual pregame, communication, and crew procedures; Applicable league, tournament, and assigning-authority policy  
**Audience:** Experienced officials admitted to the NFHS Master’s-Level Advanced Officiating pathway  
**Prerequisites:** Successful completion of RZU-NFHS-M 601 and all applicable graduate competency gates  
**Visual style:** Cinematic RefZone University film laboratory with regulation generic court geometry, iPad framing, original overlays, and no protected marks  
**Narrator style:** Calm senior officiating instructor; exact, analytical, direct, and free of theatrical exaggeration  

**Learning objectives**

- Analyze crew chief as facilitator, not dominator using current NFHS authority and original graduate-level explanation.
- Identify operative definitions, legal status, game status, primary coverage, and the exact evidence required for a defensible decision.
- Distinguish legal, illegal, borderline, and correct no-call or no-correction outcomes.
- Apply the complete penalty, clock, score, possession, reporting, and resumption sequence when applicable.
- Defend the ruling, mechanics, communication, and source choice in writing, film review, and oral examination.
- Translate the lesson into a measurable next-game, crew-leadership, evaluation, or instructional behavior.

**Production requirements**

- Actors: Three basketball officials, Eight to ten player performers as needed, One head coach, Table personnel and game administrator when applicable
- Uniforms: Unbranded regulation-style officiating uniforms, Unbranded contrasting team uniforms, Neutral table and administration attire
- Court: Regulation NFHS basketball court or original accurate digital simulation with the applicable state shot-clock configuration clearly identified
- Equipment: Basketballs, Whistles, Scoreboard and game clock, Shot clock only when the lesson identifies an authorized jurisdiction, Table equipment, Replay monitor only for authorized-policy simulations
- Graphics: Original RefZone title card, Source and review-status card, Court-coverage overlays, Clock and possession panel, Decision-pause questions, Ruling and resumption card
- Sound: Original narration, Natural court ambience, Accessible decision-pause tone, No unlicensed music

### Scene 1 — 00:00–01:05

**Learning purpose:** Frame the graduate problem and evidence standard  
**Location:** RefZone University virtual film room  
**Court configuration:** Large iPad display, source registry, and generic NFHS court model  
**Players:** None in opening frame; original play silhouettes appear on the court model  
**Officials:** Three generic high-school officials  
**Official positions:** Lead, center, and trail labels shown only when applicable  
**Camera angle:** Slow push toward the iPad player and source panel  
**Action:** The title Crew Chief as Facilitator, Not Dominator appears. A normal-speed clip or source problem plays once without commentary, followed by the course code and graduate competency.

**Narration:** Crew Chief as Facilitator, Not Dominator is a graduate-level officiating problem. The goal is not to repeat a remembered outcome. The official must identify the controlling authority, establish legal status, see the complete action, apply the correct administration, and defend the decision with professional evidence.

**On-screen text:** RZU-NFHS-M 602 · Crew Chief as Facilitator, Not Dominator

**Visual overlays:** Graduate competency; Current-source required; Normal speed first

### Scene 2 — 01:05–03:15

**Learning purpose:** Teach the controlling concepts and source map  
**Location:** Interactive rules and mechanics studio  
**Court configuration:** Split screen with source hierarchy, decision tree, and half-court diagram  
**Players:** Original diagram figures as needed  
**Officials:** Position markers matched to the lesson  
**Official positions:** Primary and secondary responsibilities highlighted  
**Camera angle:** Split screen: source panel, overhead court, and narrator inset  
**Action:** The controlling terms, conditions, exceptions, mechanics responsibilities, and administration appear one at a time. Protected publication text is not displayed.

**Narration:** Use the current official publications as authority, but explain the lesson in original language. For crew chief as facilitator, not dominator, identify every operative status, exception, coverage responsibility, and game-state consequence before selecting a ruling. The controlling references for review are 2026–27 NFHS Basketball Rules Book — current effective licensed reference; Current authorized NFHS Basketball Case Book and official interpretations; 2025–27 NFHS Basketball Officials Manual.

**On-screen text:** Authority → Status → Evidence → Classification → Administration

**Visual overlays:** Source version; Legal-status marker; Primary coverage shading; Clock and possession panel

### Scene 3 — 03:15–06:10

**Learning purpose:** Compare legal, illegal, borderline, and disciplined no-call outcomes  
**Location:** Regulation generic high-school court or accurate 3D simulation  
**Court configuration:** Four original variations of the same underlying action  
**Players:** Two teams in unbranded contrasting uniforms  
**Officials:** Three-person crew in unbranded officiating uniforms  
**Official positions:** Primary official stays visible; secondary official shown in alternate angle  
**Camera angle:** Normal-speed live angle, primary point of view, and overhead comparison  
**Action:** Four original examples play at normal speed. The legal-status moment and decisive evidence freeze briefly after each play.

**Narration:** The same visual result can come from different legal facts. In the legal example, every required condition is satisfied. In the illegal example, a prohibited act or missing condition changes the ruling. The borderline example does not establish every element from the available angle. The correct no-call or no-correction example shows disciplined restraint supported by the rule and the evidence.

**On-screen text:** LEGAL · ILLEGAL · BORDERLINE · CORRECT NO-CALL / NO-CORRECTION

**Visual overlays:** Player paths; Contact or event marker; Primary angle cone; Legal-status freeze

### Scene 4 — 06:10–09:15

**Learning purpose:** Run the original graduate case through a decision pause  
**Location:** Full-court game simulation  
**Court configuration:** Score, time, possession, team-foul, and jurisdiction panel displayed  
**Players:** Original staged or animated participants required by the case  
**Officials:** Complete crew plus table or administration personnel as needed  
**Official positions:** Calling official, crew chief, and help official clearly identified  
**Camera angle:** Normal speed, primary point of view, secondary point of view, then overhead freeze  
**Action:** Before a difficult assignment involving crew chief as facilitator, not dominator, one partner is new to the crew and another has a different local mechanics habit.

**Narration:** Watch the complete action once. Do not decide from the final reaction. Establish the legal and game status, identify the responsible official, locate the decisive evidence, and determine every penalty and resumption consequence.

**On-screen text:** DECISION PAUSE

**Visual overlays:** What is the status?; What evidence is complete?; Who owns the play?; What remains to administer?

**Decision pause:** State the controlling authority. | Identify legal and game status. | Classify the event. | Apply penalty, clock, possession, and resumption. | Prepare the professional communication.

### Scene 5 — 09:15–11:45

**Learning purpose:** Reveal, administer, and communicate the ruling  
**Location:** Court, scorer’s table, and crew-conference sequence  
**Court configuration:** Same score and clock continuity as Scene 4  
**Players:** Case participants remain consistent  
**Officials:** Calling official reports; crew chief supports the final administration  
**Official positions:** Reporting location and ensuing positions shown  
**Camera angle:** Primary official close view, table view, and wide resumption view  
**Action:** The crew chief verifies the controlling policy, invites both partners into an interactive pregame, clarifies coverage and help expectations, and creates a plan that supports rather than embarrasses either official.

**Narration:** The graduate ruling is: The crew chief verifies the controlling policy, invites both partners into an interactive pregame, clarifies coverage and help expectations, and creates a plan that supports rather than embarrasses either official. The official must also confirm the full game state and avoid adding a penalty, correction, or explanation that the active authority does not support.

**Dialogue:** Let us agree on the authorized mechanic, identify where help is appropriate, and make sure each partner knows how we will communicate if the play becomes unusual.

**On-screen text:** RULING · AUTHORITY · PENALTY · CLOCK · POSSESSION · RESUMPTION

**Visual overlays:** Ruling card; Signal sequence; Clock result; Possession and restart

**Ruling reveal:** The crew chief verifies the controlling policy, invites both partners into an interactive pregame, clarifies coverage and help expectations, and creates a plan that supports rather than embarrasses either official.

### Scene 6 — 11:45–14:30

**Learning purpose:** Complete the graduate assignment and next-game application  
**Location:** RefZone University film laboratory and portfolio workspace  
**Court configuration:** Normal-speed replay beside assignment checklist  
**Players:** Case clip repeats at normal speed  
**Officials:** Primary official angle remains labeled  
**Official positions:** Correct position and alternative open angle compared  
**Camera angle:** Side-by-side film, decision tree, and portfolio prompt  
**Action:** The play returns to normal speed. The learner records the source, ruling, mechanics, communication, and one measurable next-game behavior.

**Narration:** Mastery of crew chief as facilitator, not dominator requires written analysis, oral defense, and applied evidence. Complete the source-controlled case memorandum, film tag, communication rehearsal, and next-game performance goal. Review any uncertain rule or local policy with a qualified reviewer before instruction or publication.

**On-screen text:** SOURCE · FILM · ORAL DEFENSE · NEXT-GAME APPLICATION

**Visual overlays:** Assignment checklist; Rubric anchors; Review status; Next lesson

**Accessibility and delivery**

- Captions: Create a synchronized WebVTT caption file from the approved narration and dialogue.
- Transcript: Provide a searchable transcript with scene timestamps, source labels, and decision-pause markers.
- Audio description: Describe court location, player and official movement, scoreboard data, signals, diagrams, and overlays not conveyed by narration.
- Thumbnail: RefZone University iPad frame with an original high-school basketball scene illustrating Crew Chief as Facilitator, Not Dominator; accessible high-contrast title; no league, school, player, or publisher marks.

**Quality-control checklist**

- Validate every rule, penalty, mechanics responsibility, timing consequence, and local-policy distinction with qualified reviewers.
- Use only original RefZone narration, scenarios, dialogue, diagrams, assessments, and overlays.
- Do not reproduce protected rulebook text, casebook wording, mechanics diagrams, licensed footage, logos, or real-person likenesses without authorization.
- Maintain regulation court geometry, stable player numbers, consistent direction, physically realistic movement, accurate signals, and correct crew positions.
- Identify shot-clock and replay content as jurisdiction-specific when not part of the universal NFHS playing rules.
- Provide captions, searchable transcript, audio description, keyboard support, reduced-motion alternatives, and high-contrast graphics.
