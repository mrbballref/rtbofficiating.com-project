## Lesson 1: 24-second control and operator duties

- Lesson ID: `fibm-f132-l01-24-second-control-and-operator-duties`
- Video title: 24-second control and operator duties
- Target audience: Developing FIBA men’s officials
- Estimated duration: 10:45
- Rule/source references: FIBA Official Basketball Rules — violations and game administration; FIBA Refereeing Material Library and authorized iRef instructional resources — EB Official Interpretations and guidelines; Current technical rules clarification and assignment directive

### Learning objectives
- Explain 24-second control and operator duties in original RefZone University language and identify the controlling FIBA or authorized event authority.
- Identify the FIBA season, internal regulations, Official Basketball Rules and authorized competition regulations, source version, effective date, and assignment directive before applying the concept.
- Distinguish a supported legal outcome, an illegal or unsupported outcome, a borderline or unresolved outcome, and a disciplined no-call, no-correction, or withhold-conclusion outcome.
- Apply the correct mechanics, evidence standard, penalty, clock, shot clock, possession, replay, resumption, research, or policy process when applicable.
- Communicate the conclusion briefly, calmly, accurately, neutrally, and without importing terminology from another ruleset.
- Review the work on film or through source evidence and record a measurable next-game, teaching, leadership, or research application.

**Learning purpose:** Frame the authority, effective date, and learning question
**Location:** RefZone University digital classroom and source-control desk

**Officials:** Learner, instructor, and qualified reviewer represented without real-person likenesses
**Official positions:** Not applicable
**Camera angle:** Slow push-in to source timeline and course map

**Action:** The screen identifies 24-second control and operator duties, the applicable event type, and the difference between current-effective, future-effective, and event-specific authority.

**Narration:** This lesson examines 24-second control and operator duties. Begin by identifying the FIBA season, controlling internal regulations, Official Basketball Rules and authorized competition regulations, officiating manual, official interpretation or technical clarification, and assignment directive. FIBA supplies the underlying playing code, but FIBA competition implementations and competition procedures control when they differ. Never apply a future-effective FIBA change or a domestic-league procedure without confirmed FIBA adoption.

### Scene 3 — 03:00-05:10

**Location:** Same court with four original play variations

**Camera angle:** Four-panel normal-speed comparison with no slow motion initially

**Action:** Four original examples isolate one decisive fact for 24-second control and operator duties: a legal outcome, an illegal outcome, a borderline view, and a correct no-call or no-correction.

**Narration:** Compare the plays at normal speed. Do not react only to the final result. For 24-second control and operator duties, locate the first relevant action, identify legal status before it, and decide whether the evidence completes every required element. A dramatic result does not replace rule evidence.

**On-screen text:** See the first relevant fact

**Ruling reveal:** The ruling follows the complete legal-status and evidence sequence, not appearance, reputation, crowd reaction, or expected outcome.

### Scene 4 — 05:10-07:30

**Officials:** Calling official, partners, table officials, commissioner, and replay personnel when authorized
**Official positions:** Open angle contrasted with a straight-lined or late position
**Camera angle:** Primary POV, secondary POV, overhead diagram, freeze at status, freeze at decisive fact

**Action:** The play runs at normal speed, freezes only to verify facts, returns to normal speed, and completes reporting and resumption for 24-second control and operator duties.

**Narration:** Positioning is part of the decision. For 24-second control and operator duties, establish the matchup, maintain an open look, observe the complete action, and avoid reaching from a secondary position. After the whistle or administrative decision, confirm player, ball, clock, shot-clock, team-foul, possession, replay, and resumption status.
