## Lesson 1: Journal and practitioner audiences

- Lesson ID: `fibw-e752-l01-journal-and-practitioner-audiences`
- Video title: Journal and practitioner audiences
- Target audience: Doctoral learners, supervisors, researchers, instructors, and policy leaders
- Estimated duration: 10:45
- Rule/source references: Validated FIBA and FIBA source registry; Original RefZone University research-methods and academic-integrity framework; Applicable institutional research ethics, privacy, licensing, and restricted-data requirements

### Learning objectives
- Explain journal and practitioner audiences in original RefZone University language and identify the controlling FIBA or authorized event authority.
- Identify the FIBA season, internal regulations, Official Basketball Rules and authorized competition regulations, source version, effective date, and assignment directive before applying the concept.
- Distinguish supported, unsupported, unresolved, and ethically withheld conclusions using a defensible evidence standard.
- Apply the correct mechanics, evidence standard, penalty, clock, shot clock, possession, replay, resumption, research, or policy process when applicable.
- Communicate the conclusion briefly, calmly, accurately, neutrally, and without importing terminology from another ruleset.
- Review the work on film or through source evidence and record a measurable next-game, teaching, leadership, or research application.

### Scene 1 — 00:00-01:20
**Learning purpose:** Frame the authority, effective date, and learning question
**Location:** RefZone University digital classroom and source-control desk
**Court configuration:** Split view of the iPad player, source timeline, and assignment card
**Players:** None
**Officials:** Learner, instructor, and qualified reviewer represented without real-person likenesses
**Official positions:** Not applicable
**Camera angle:** Slow push-in to source timeline and course map

**Action:** The screen identifies Journal and practitioner audiences, the applicable event type, and the difference between current-effective, future-effective, and event-specific authority.

**Narration:** This lesson examines Journal and practitioner audiences. Begin by identifying the FIBA season, controlling internal regulations, Official Basketball Rules and authorized competition regulations, officiating manual, official interpretation or technical clarification, and assignment directive. FIBA supplies the underlying playing code, but FIBA competition implementations and competition procedures control when they differ. Never apply a future-effective FIBA change or a domestic-league procedure without confirmed FIBA adoption.

**On-screen text:** Competition → Source → Effective date → Ruling

**Dialogue:** Instructor: “Name the controlling event document before you analyze the play.”

**Visual overlays:**
- FIBA assignment type
- Current-effective source
- Future-effective source
- Human-review gate

**Decision pause:**
- Which competition is being officiated?
- Which rule code is expressly adopted?
- Is the source current effective or future effective?

### Scene 3 — 03:00-05:10
**Learning purpose:** Compare legal, illegal, borderline, and disciplined no-action outcomes
**Location:** Same court with four original play variations
**Court configuration:** Identical score, clock, players, and direction for controlled comparison
**Players:** Same generic participants and jersey numbers in every variation
**Officials:** Same three-person crew
**Official positions:** Primary and secondary angles remain consistent
**Camera angle:** Four-panel normal-speed comparison with no slow motion initially

**Action:** Four original examples isolate one decisive fact for Journal and practitioner audiences: a legal outcome, an illegal outcome, a borderline view, and a correct no-call or no-correction.

**Narration:** Compare the plays at normal speed. Do not react only to the final result. For Journal and practitioner audiences, locate the first relevant action, identify legal status before it, and decide whether the evidence completes every required element. A dramatic result does not replace rule evidence.

**On-screen text:** Supported · Unsupported · Unresolved · Withhold conclusion

**Dialogue:** Partner: “My angle does not complete the missing element. Stay with the ruling unless authorized evidence changes it.”

**Visual overlays:**
- LEGAL
- ILLEGAL
- BORDERLINE
- NO-CALL / NO-CORRECTION

**Decision pause:**
- What single fact separates the examples?
- Is the available angle complete enough to rule?

**Ruling reveal:** The ruling follows the complete legal-status and evidence sequence, not appearance, reputation, crowd reaction, or expected outcome.

### Scene 4 — 05:10-07:30
**Learning purpose:** Apply mechanics, coverage, and administration
**Location:** Court, scorer’s table, and replay station when applicable
**Court configuration:** Primary official angle, secondary official angle, overhead view, and restart location
**Players:** Same participants
**Officials:** Calling official, partners, table officials, commissioner, and replay personnel when authorized
**Official positions:** Open angle contrasted with a straight-lined or late position
**Camera angle:** Primary POV, secondary POV, overhead diagram, freeze at status, freeze at decisive fact

**Action:** The play runs at normal speed, freezes only to verify facts, returns to normal speed, and completes reporting and resumption for Journal and practitioner audiences.

**Narration:** Positioning is part of the decision. For Journal and practitioner audiences, establish the matchup, maintain an open look, observe the complete action, and avoid reaching from a secondary position. After the whistle or administrative decision, confirm player, ball, clock, shot-clock, team-foul, possession, replay, and resumption status.

**On-screen text:** Angle → Evidence → Classification → Administration

**Dialogue:** Calling official: “I have the complete action. Confirm the clock and restart.”

**Visual overlays:**
- Primary coverage
- Secondary help
- Open angle
- Clock and shot clock
- Penalty and restart

**Decision pause:**
- Who owns the play?
- What help is factual and permitted?
- What is the complete administration?

**Ruling reveal:** Use the authorized signal, report accurately, restore every required game condition, and resume without unnecessary delay.

### Scene 5 — 07:30-09:10
**Learning purpose:** Demonstrate professional communication and leadership
**Location:** Court, coach communication lane, crew conference, and postgame reporting room
**Court configuration:** Dead-ball control and protected restart space
**Players:** Players remain in correct positions
**Officials:** Calling official, crew chief, partners, table, and supervisor
**Official positions:** One official communicates while partners secure the court and restart
**Camera angle:** Table-facing medium shot, coach-side angle, and crew huddle close-up

**Action:** The crew communicates the decision for Journal and practitioner audiences briefly, neutrally, and with source-based accuracy.

**Narration:** Communication does not replace accuracy, but it protects game flow and trust. State only the information needed for Journal and practitioner audiences. Do not import terminology from another ruleset, debate indefinitely, criticize a partner, or promise an outcome.

**On-screen text:** Information, not argument

**Dialogue:** Researcher: “The evidence supports this limited conclusion; broader claims require additional data and review.”

**Visual overlays:**
- Accurate
- Brief
- Calm
- Neutral
- Next action

**Decision pause:**

**Learning purpose:** Complete assessment, reflection, and next-game transfer
**Location:** RefZone film room and accessible iPad course interface

**Players:** None
**Officials:** Learner reviewing the complete sequence
**Official positions:** Primary-coverage diagram remains visible
**Camera angle:** iPad close-up with keyboard, caption, transcript, and audio-description controls

**Action:** The learner answers the decision pause, explains Journal and practitioner audiences aloud, saves a note, and defines a measurable next-assignment behavior.

**Narration:** Review Journal and practitioner audiences at normal speed, use slow motion only to verify facts, and return to normal speed before judging the whistle. Cite the controlling source, explain the decision in original language, administer the entire result, and record one behavior to apply on the next assignment.

**On-screen text:** Study it. See it. Rule it. Administer it. Review it.

**Visual overlays:**

### Accessibility and production controls
- Captions: Create synchronized WebVTT captions from the approved narration and dialogue.
- Transcript: Create a searchable transcript with scene timestamps, source labels, and effective-date markers.
- Audio description: Describe court location, player and official movement, signals, score, clocks, diagrams, freeze frames, and overlays not conveyed in narration.
- Thumbnail: RefZone University iPad frame showing an original unbranded FIBA women’s development scene for Journal and practitioner audiences; no FIBA, FIBA, team, athlete, or broadcast marks.

- Verify the controlling event rules code and effective date before production.
- Validate every rule, interpretation, penalty, clock, shot-clock, replay, and mechanics statement with qualified reviewers.
- Use only original RefZone scenarios, narration, diagrams, assessments, and dialogue.
- Maintain regulation geometry, consistent uniforms, numbers, direction, score, clocks, and possession.
- Reject distorted anatomy, ball movement, court lines, signals, mechanics, or impossible action.
- Use no unlicensed footage, logos, music, broadcasts, real-person likenesses, protected rulebook text, interpretations, or mechanics diagrams.
- Provide captions, searchable transcript, audio description, keyboard controls, reduced-motion alternatives, and high-contrast graphics.
