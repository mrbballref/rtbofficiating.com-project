**Program:** NCAA Division I Men’s Basketball Officiating Master’s-Level Advanced Officiating Program  
**Format:** Platform-agnostic text-to-video, image-to-video, animation, virtual production, traditional camera, editing, captioning, and LMS package

## Graduate Contact-Judgment Framework for NCAA Men’s Basketball

**Estimated duration:** 15 minutes core video plus graduate source work, case or film laboratory, applied artifact, and oral defense  
**Rule references:** NCAA Men’s Basketball Rule 4 definitions and Rule 10 foul classifications; Current NCAA men’s approved rulings, interpretations, and points of emphasis; Current authorized NCAA men’s mechanics and officiating philosophy resources; 2026-27 point-of-emphasis directives for post play, offensive three seconds, freedom of movement, and traveling  
**Audience:** Experienced NCAA Division I men’s officials, aspiring crew chiefs, evaluators, observers, and graduate-pathway candidates  
**Prerequisites:** RZU-D1M-M 502  
**Visual style:** Original RefZone University cinematic collegiate-basketball instruction with an iPad lesson frame, regulation generic court, restrained telestration, and no unlicensed marks  
**Narrator style:** Calm, precise, authoritative, evidence-based, and professionally neutral  

**Learning objectives**

- Analyze graduate contact-judgment framework for ncaa men’s basketball using current NCAA men’s playing-rules authority and applicable NCAA Division I administrative authority.
- Identify operative legal status, game status, crew responsibility, and the exact evidence required for a defensible decision.
- Distinguish supported, unsupported, borderline, and disciplined no-action outcomes.
- Apply the complete penalty, clock, shot-clock, timeout, challenge, possession, reporting, and resumption sequence when applicable.
- Defend the conclusion, mechanics, communication, and source choice through original writing, film review, applied evidence, and oral examination.
- Translate the lesson into a measurable next-game, crew-leadership, evaluation, or instructional behavior.

**Production requirements**

- Actors: Three officials, Two teams of unbranded players, Head coaches and bench personnel as needed, Table crew, Replay or evaluator personnel when required
- Uniforms: Stable unbranded officiating uniforms, Contrasting unbranded team uniforms, Consistent player numbers and direction of play
- Court: Regulation NCAA men’s court geometry with correct markings, benches, table, game clock, 30-second shot clocks, and replay station when applicable
- Equipment: Basketballs, Whistles, Game and shot-clock displays, Scorer and timer materials, Replay monitor or simulated evidence workstation when applicable
- Graphics: Rule and source citation cards, Primary and secondary coverage shading, Player and ball paths, Contact or event markers, Clock, shot-clock, timeout, media, and challenge panels, Decision-pause and conclusion cards
- Sound: Original narration, Original dialogue, Clean whistle and court ambience, No unlicensed music or broadcast audio

### Scene 1 — 00:00–01:20

**Learning purpose:** Establish the graduate problem, game context, and learning objectives  
**Location:** RefZone University virtual graduate seminar and regulation generic collegiate court  
**Court configuration:** NCAA men’s court markings, 30-second shot clocks, game clock, benches, table, and replay station when applicable  
**Players:** Original unbranded collegiate-level players or accurate 3D simulations  
**Officials:** Three-person crew in unbranded professional officiating uniforms  
**Official positions:** Lead, center, and trail labels appear before the action begins  
**Camera angle:** Cinematic iPad lesson frame, broadcast wide, and primary-official point of view  
**Action:** An original opening sequence introduces Graduate Contact-Judgment Framework for NCAA Men’s Basketball. The score, time, shot clock, team fouls, timeout status, and challenge status are displayed consistently.

**Narration:** Graduate Contact-Judgment Framework for NCAA Men’s Basketball is not mastered by memorizing a result. Graduate mastery requires current authority, legal and game status, complete evidence, correct crew ownership, full administration, concise communication, and a defensible normal-speed film process.

**On-screen text:** RZU-D1M-M 511 · Graduate Contact-Judgment Framework for NCAA Men’s Basketball

**Visual overlays:** Learning objectives; Source season; Game-state panel; Graduate competency gate

### Scene 2 — 01:20–03:35

**Learning purpose:** Teach the controlling framework and floor-observation sequence  
**Location:** Split-screen source laboratory and overhead court diagram  
**Court configuration:** Court zones, primary and secondary coverage, clock and shot-clock responsibilities highlighted  
**Players:** Original diagram figures and game-action inserts  
**Officials:** Crew positions remain labeled throughout  
**Official positions:** Primary official, potential help official, crew chief, and table responsibility identified  
**Camera angle:** Split screen: source panel, overhead tactical view, and narrator inset  
**Action:** The current source hierarchy, operative status, decisive visual evidence, mechanics responsibilities, and administration sequence appear one at a time. Protected publication text is not displayed.

**Narration:** Use official publications as authority while teaching in original RefZone language. For graduate contact-judgment framework for ncaa men’s basketball, identify the controlling terms, exact visual or administrative evidence, crew responsibility, penalty, clock, shot clock, possession, review, reporting, and resumption. Primary references include NCAA Men’s Basketball Rule 4 definitions and Rule 10 foul classifications; Current NCAA men’s approved rulings, interpretations, and points of emphasis; Current authorized NCAA men’s mechanics and officiating philosophy resources.

**On-screen text:** AUTHORITY → STATUS → EVIDENCE → OWNERSHIP → ADMINISTRATION

**Visual overlays:** Source version; Legal-status marker; Primary coverage shading; Clock, shot clock, and challenge panel

### Scene 3 — 03:35–06:35

**Learning purpose:** Compare supported, unsupported, borderline, and disciplined no-action outcomes  
**Location:** Regulation generic collegiate court or accurate 3D simulation  
**Court configuration:** Four original variations of the same lesson concept  
**Players:** Two teams in stable unbranded contrasting uniforms  
**Officials:** Three-person crew with consistent positions and jersey numbers  
**Official positions:** Primary official visible; secondary angle shown only after the normal-speed sequence  
**Camera angle:** Normal-speed live angle, primary point of view, secondary point of view, and overhead freeze  
**Action:** Four original examples play at normal speed. Each then freezes at the legal-status or administrative fact that controls the outcome.

**Narration:** The supported example satisfies every required element. The unsupported example contains a prohibited act, missing prerequisite, wrong authority, or incomplete administration. The borderline example does not establish every element. The disciplined no-action example shows why a correct no-call, no-correction, no-review, or withheld approval can best preserve the rule and the integrity of the process.

**On-screen text:** SUPPORTED · UNSUPPORTED · BORDERLINE · DISCIPLINED NO-ACTION

**Visual overlays:** Player or evidence paths; Decisive event marker; Primary angle cone; Legal-status or review-status freeze

### Scene 4 — 06:35–09:40

**Learning purpose:** Run an original graduate case through a decision pause  
**Location:** Full-court game simulation, film laboratory, or crew-conference setting  
**Court configuration:** Score, time, shot clock, possession, team fouls, timeout, media, challenge, and jurisdiction data remain visible  
**Players:** Original staged or animated participants required by the case  
**Officials:** Complete crew, table, replay, evaluator, or administration personnel as needed  
**Official positions:** Calling official, crew chief, help official, and table responsibility clearly identified  
**Camera angle:** Normal speed, primary point of view, secondary point of view, then overhead or evidence freeze  
**Action:** In an original normal-speed play illustrating “Graduate Contact-Judgment Framework for NCAA Men’s Basketball,” the primary official sees contact but the result, crowd response, and alternate angle create pressure for a whistle or upgrade.

**Narration:** Watch the complete action once. Do not decide from the final result or strongest reaction. Establish the legal and game status, identify the controlling authority, locate the decisive evidence, confirm ownership, and determine every administration consequence.

**On-screen text:** DECISION PAUSE

**Visual overlays:** What is the status?; What authority controls?; Which official owns the decision?; What remains to administer?

**Decision pause:** State the controlling authority and jurisdiction. | Identify legal, game, clock, shot-clock, and challenge status. | Classify the event or competency result. | Apply penalty, review, possession, and resumption. | Prepare the professional communication or defense.

### Scene 5 — 09:40–12:10

**Learning purpose:** Reveal, administer, and communicate the conclusion  
**Location:** Court, scorer’s table, monitor, evaluation room, or oral-defense panel  
**Court configuration:** Same game-state and evidence continuity as Scene 4  
**Players:** Case participants remain visually consistent  
**Officials:** Calling official or candidate presents; crew chief or reviewer confirms administration  
**Official positions:** Reporting location, monitor position, conference geometry, or defense panel shown accurately  
**Camera angle:** Primary close view, table or panel view, and wide resumption or conclusion view  
**Action:** Establish legal status before contact, identify the initiator and point of contact, evaluate the complete effect and safety risk, confirm primary ownership, and select the ruleset-supported foul classification or correct no-call.

**Narration:** The graduate conclusion is: Establish legal status before contact, identify the initiator and point of contact, evaluate the complete effect and safety risk, confirm primary ownership, and select the ruleset-supported foul classification or correct no-call. The candidate must also state what may not be inferred, reviewed, corrected, penalized, or approved from the available evidence.

**Dialogue:** The defender was legal before the contact, and the available contact did not create the prohibited effect required for a foul. That is why the crew stayed with the no-call.

**On-screen text:** CONCLUSION · AUTHORITY · ADMINISTRATION · COMMUNICATION · RESUMPTION

**Visual overlays:** Ruling or competency card; Penalty and review result; Clock and shot-clock result; Possession, restart, or remediation

**Conclusion reveal:** Establish legal status before contact, identify the initiator and point of contact, evaluate the complete effect and safety risk, confirm primary ownership, and select the ruleset-supported foul classification or correct no-call.

### Scene 6 — 12:10–15:00

**Learning purpose:** Complete graduate assessment, film review, and next-game application  
**Location:** RefZone University film laboratory and portfolio workspace  
**Court configuration:** Normal-speed replay or evidence set beside assignment and rubric panels  
**Players:** The original case repeats at normal speed when applicable  
**Officials:** Primary official angle and crew positions remain labeled  
**Official positions:** Correct position, alternative angle, or evaluator viewpoint compared  
**Camera angle:** Side-by-side film, decision tree, rubric, and portfolio prompt  
**Action:** The learner records the source, status, ruling, mechanics, administration, communication, evidence limitation, and one measurable applied-performance behavior.

**Narration:** Mastery of graduate contact-judgment framework for ncaa men’s basketball requires original written analysis, normal-speed film work, oral defense, and applied evidence. Complete the source memorandum, case or performance artifact, communication rehearsal, and next-game goal. Route uncertain rules, NCAA Division I conference and championship procedures, mechanics, or review questions to qualified human review before instruction or publication.

**On-screen text:** SOURCE · FILM · APPLIED EVIDENCE · ORAL DEFENSE · NEXT-GAME TARGET

**Visual overlays:** Assignment checklist; Rubric anchors; Review status; Next lesson
