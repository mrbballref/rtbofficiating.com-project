## Lesson 1: Normal-speed first viewing

- Lesson ID: `fibw-a531-l01-normal-speed-first-viewing`
- Video title: Normal-speed first viewing
- Target audience: Advanced FIBA women’s officials, crew chiefs, and evaluators
- Estimated duration: 10:45
- Rule/source references: FIBA Internal Regulations and authorized event competition regulations — Technical Rules Clarification and Instant Replay appendix; FIBA Refereeing Material Library and authorized iRef instructional resources — IRS protocols and referee responsibilities; FIBA Official Basketball Rules — protest, correctable errors, and IRS foundation

### Learning objectives
- Explain normal-speed first viewing in original RefZone University language and identify the controlling FIBA or authorized event authority.
- Identify the FIBA season, internal regulations, Official Basketball Rules and authorized competition regulations, source version, effective date, and assignment directive before applying the concept.
- Distinguish a supported legal outcome, an illegal or unsupported outcome, a borderline or unresolved outcome, and a disciplined no-call, no-correction, or withhold-conclusion outcome.
- Apply the correct mechanics, evidence standard, penalty, clock, shot clock, possession, replay, resumption, research, or policy process when applicable.
- Communicate the conclusion briefly, calmly, accurately, neutrally, and without importing terminology from another ruleset.
- Review the work on film or through source evidence and record a measurable next-game, teaching, leadership, or research application.

### Scene 1 — 00:00-01:20
**Learning purpose:** Frame the authority, effective date, and learning question
**Location:** RefZone University digital classroom and source-control desk
**Court configuration:** Split view of the iPad player, source timeline, and assignment card
**Players:** None
**Officials:** Learner, instructor, and qualified reviewer represented without real-person likenesses
**Official positions:** Not applicable
**Camera angle:** Slow push-in to source timeline and course map

**Action:** The screen identifies Normal-speed first viewing, the applicable event type, and the difference between current-effective, future-effective, and event-specific authority.

**Narration:** This lesson examines Normal-speed first viewing. Begin by identifying the FIBA season, controlling internal regulations, Official Basketball Rules and authorized competition regulations, officiating manual, official interpretation or technical clarification, and assignment directive. FIBA supplies the underlying playing code, but FIBA competition implementations and competition procedures control when they differ. Never apply a future-effective FIBA change or a domestic-league procedure without confirmed FIBA adoption.

**On-screen text:** Competition → Source → Effective date → Ruling

**Dialogue:** Instructor: “Name the controlling event document before you analyze the play.”

**Visual overlays:**
- FIBA assignment type
- Current-effective source
- Future-effective source
- Human-review gate

**Decision pause:**
- Which competition is being officiated?
- Which rule code is expressly adopted?
- Is the source current effective or future effective?

### Scene 2 — 01:20-03:00
**Learning purpose:** Teach the governing concept in original RefZone language
**Location:** Unbranded regulation basketball court and digital tactics board
**Court configuration:** Court geometry and equipment match the selected event code
**Players:** Generic men’s teams in consistent unbranded uniforms
**Officials:** Three-person crew and table officials
**Official positions:** Lead, trail, centre, scorer, timer, shot-clock operator, and commissioner when applicable
**Camera angle:** Broadcast sideline moving to overhead tactical view

**Action:** An original staged sequence illustrates the legal status and governing conditions for Normal-speed first viewing.

**Narration:** The governing concept is not memorized as isolated wording. For Normal-speed first viewing, identify the defined status, location, timing, player responsibility, official responsibility, exception, penalty, and restart. The goal is to recognize the complete condition set in real time.

**On-screen text:** Status · Location · Timing · Responsibility · Exception

**Dialogue:** Crew chief: “We are applying the published condition, not a familiar habit from another code.”

**Visual overlays:**
- Defined status
- Player and ball location
- Game clock
- Shot clock
- Primary coverage

- Which exception could change the result?

### Scene 3 — 03:00-05:10
**Learning purpose:** Compare legal, illegal, borderline, and disciplined no-action outcomes
**Location:** Same court with four original play variations
**Court configuration:** Identical score, clock, players, and direction for controlled comparison
**Players:** Same generic participants and jersey numbers in every variation
**Officials:** Same three-person crew
**Official positions:** Primary and secondary angles remain consistent
**Camera angle:** Four-panel normal-speed comparison with no slow motion initially

**Action:** Four original examples isolate one decisive fact for Normal-speed first viewing: a legal outcome, an illegal outcome, a borderline view, and a correct no-call or no-correction.

**Narration:** Compare the plays at normal speed. Do not react only to the final result. For Normal-speed first viewing, locate the first relevant action, identify legal status before it, and decide whether the evidence completes every required element. A dramatic result does not replace rule evidence.

**On-screen text:** See the first relevant fact

**Dialogue:** Partner: “My angle does not complete the missing element. Stay with the ruling unless authorized evidence changes it.”

**Visual overlays:**
- LEGAL
- ILLEGAL
- BORDERLINE
- NO-CALL / NO-CORRECTION

**Decision pause:**
- What single fact separates the examples?
- Is the available angle complete enough to rule?

**Ruling reveal:** The ruling follows the complete legal-status and evidence sequence, not appearance, reputation, crowd reaction, or expected outcome.

### Scene 4 — 05:10-07:30
**Learning purpose:** Apply mechanics, coverage, and administration
**Location:** Court, scorer’s table, and replay station when applicable
**Court configuration:** Primary official angle, secondary official angle, overhead view, and restart location
**Players:** Same participants
**Officials:** Calling official, partners, table officials, commissioner, and replay personnel when authorized
**Official positions:** Open angle contrasted with a straight-lined or late position
**Camera angle:** Primary POV, secondary POV, overhead diagram, freeze at status, freeze at decisive fact

**Action:** The play runs at normal speed, freezes only to verify facts, returns to normal speed, and completes reporting and resumption for Normal-speed first viewing.

**Narration:** Positioning is part of the decision. For Normal-speed first viewing, establish the matchup, maintain an open look, observe the complete action, and avoid reaching from a secondary position. After the whistle or administrative decision, confirm player, ball, clock, shot-clock, team-foul, possession, replay, and resumption status.

**On-screen text:** Angle → Evidence → Classification → Administration

**Dialogue:** Calling official: “I have the complete action. Confirm the clock and restart.”

**Visual overlays:**
- Primary coverage
- Secondary help
- Open angle
- Clock and shot clock
- Penalty and restart

**Decision pause:**
- Who owns the play?
- What help is factual and permitted?
- What is the complete administration?

**Ruling reveal:** Use the authorized signal, report accurately, restore every required game condition, and resume without unnecessary delay.

### Scene 5 — 07:30-09:10
**Learning purpose:** Demonstrate professional communication and leadership
**Location:** Court, coach communication lane, crew conference, and postgame reporting room
**Court configuration:** Dead-ball control and protected restart space
**Players:** Players remain in correct positions
**Officials:** Calling official, crew chief, partners, table, and supervisor
**Official positions:** One official communicates while partners secure the court and restart
**Camera angle:** Table-facing medium shot, coach-side angle, and crew huddle close-up

**Action:** The crew communicates the decision for Normal-speed first viewing briefly, neutrally, and with source-based accuracy.

**Narration:** Communication does not replace accuracy, but it protects game flow and trust. State only the information needed for Normal-speed first viewing. Do not import terminology from another ruleset, debate indefinitely, criticize a partner, or promise an outcome.

**On-screen text:** Information, not argument

**Dialogue:** Official: “The ruling is based on the event’s controlling rule and the observed status. We will resume at the designated location with the corrected clock.”

**Visual overlays:**

- Brief

Cite the controlling source, explain the decision in original language, administer the entire result, and record one behavior to apply on the next assignment.

- Thumbnail: RefZone University iPad frame showing an original unbranded FIBA women’s development scene for Normal-speed first viewing; no FIBA, FIBA, team, athlete, or broadcast marks.

- Verify the controlling event rules code and effective date before production.
- Validate every rule, interpretation, penalty, clock, shot-clock, replay, and mechanics statement with qualified reviewers.
- Use only original RefZone scenarios, narration, diagrams, assessments, and dialogue.
- Maintain regulation geometry, consistent uniforms, numbers, direction, score, clocks, and possession.
- Reject distorted anatomy, ball movement, court lines, signals, mechanics, or impossible action.
- Use no unlicensed footage, logos, music, broadcasts, real-person likenesses, protected rulebook text, interpretations, or mechanics diagrams.
- Provide captions, searchable transcript, audio description, keyboard controls, reduced-motion alternatives, and high-contrast graphics.
