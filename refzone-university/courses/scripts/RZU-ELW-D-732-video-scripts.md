## Officiating department governance

**Estimated duration:** 10:45  
**Target audience:** Doctoral learners, supervisors, researchers, instructors, and policy leaders  
**Rule/source map:** FIBA Official Basketball Rules — teams and playing regulations; 2026-27 EuroLeague Women Regulations and In-Arena Code of Conduct

### Scene 1 — 00:00-01:20
**Learning purpose:** Frame the authority, effective date, and learning question  
**Location:** RefZone University digital classroom and source-control desk  
**Camera:** Slow push-in to source timeline and course map  
**Action:** The screen identifies Officiating department governance, the applicable event type, and the difference between current-effective, future-effective, and event-specific authority.  
**Narration:** This lesson examines Officiating department governance. Begin by identifying the EuroLeague season, controlling bylaws, adapted rules, authorized FIBA mechanics guidance, technical and assignment clarification, and assignment directive. FIBA supplies the underlying playing code, but EuroLeague Women competition regulations and authorized event modifications and competition procedures control when they differ. Never apply a future-effective FIBA change or a domestic-league procedure without confirmed EuroLeague adoption.  
**Dialogue:** Instructor: “Name the controlling event document before you analyze the play.”  
**On-screen text:** Competition → Source → Effective date → Ruling  
**Overlays:** FIBA Europe assignment type, Current-effective source, Future-effective source, Human-review gate  
**Decision pause:** Which competition is being officiated? | Which rule code is expressly adopted? | Is the source current effective or future effective?

### Scene 3 — 03:00-05:10
**Learning purpose:** Compare legal, illegal, borderline, and disciplined no-action outcomes  
**Location:** Same court with four original play variations  
**Camera:** Four-panel normal-speed comparison with no slow motion initially  
**Action:** Four original examples isolate one decisive fact for Officiating department governance: a legal outcome, an illegal outcome, a borderline view, and a correct no-call or no-correction.  
**Narration:** Compare the plays at normal speed. Do not react only to the final result. For Officiating department governance, locate the first relevant action, identify legal status before it, and decide whether the evidence completes every required element. A dramatic result does not replace rule evidence.  
**Dialogue:** Partner: “My angle does not complete the missing element. Stay with the ruling unless authorized evidence changes it.”  
**On-screen text:** Supported · Unsupported · Unresolved · Withhold conclusion

### Scene 4 — 05:10-07:30
**Learning purpose:** Apply mechanics, coverage, and administration

**Camera:** Primary POV, secondary POV, overhead diagram, freeze at status, freeze at decisive fact

**Narration:** Positioning is part of the decision. For Officiating department governance, establish the matchup, maintain an open look, observe the complete action, and avoid reaching from a secondary position. After the whistle or administrative decision, confirm player, ball, clock, shot-clock, team-foul, possession, replay, and resumption status.
