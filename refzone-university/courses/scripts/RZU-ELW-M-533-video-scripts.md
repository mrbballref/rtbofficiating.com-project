## Advanced rules and adapted-rules examination

**Estimated duration:** 10:45  
**Target audience:** Advanced EuroLeague Women’s officials, crew chiefs, and evaluators  
**Rule/source map:** 2025-26 FIBA Europe FIBA mechanics and game-official guidance — adapted rules, casebook, guidelines, and protocols; 2026-27 EuroLeague Women Regulations — technical rules, competition regulations, disciplinary code, and IRS appendices

### Scene 1 — 00:00-01:20
**Learning purpose:** Frame the authority, effective date, and learning question  
**Location:** RefZone University digital classroom and source-control desk  
**Camera:** Slow push-in to source timeline and course map  
**Action:** The screen identifies Advanced rules and adapted-rules examination, the applicable event type, and the difference between current-effective, future-effective, and event-specific authority.  
**Narration:** This lesson examines Advanced rules and adapted-rules examination. Begin by identifying the EuroLeague season, controlling bylaws, adapted rules, authorized FIBA mechanics guidance, technical and assignment clarification, and assignment directive. FIBA supplies the underlying playing code, but EuroLeague Women competition regulations and authorized event modifications and competition procedures control when they differ. Never apply a future-effective FIBA change or a domestic-league procedure without confirmed EuroLeague adoption.  
**Dialogue:** Instructor: “Name the controlling event document before you analyze the play.”  
**On-screen text:** Competition → Source → Effective date → Ruling  
**Overlays:** FIBA Europe assignment type, Current-effective source, Future-effective source, Human-review gate  
**Decision pause:** Which competition is being officiated? | Which rule code is expressly adopted? | Is the source current effective or future effective?  
**Ruling reveal:** 

### Scene 2 — 01:20-03:00
**Learning purpose:** Teach the governing concept in original RefZone language  
**Location:** Unbranded regulation basketball court and digital tactics board  
**Camera:** Broadcast sideline moving to overhead tactical view  
**Action:** An original staged sequence illustrates the legal status and governing conditions for Advanced rules and adapted-rules examination.  
**Narration:** The governing concept is not memorized as isolated wording. For Advanced rules and adapted-rules examination, identify the defined status, location, timing, player responsibility, official responsibility, exception, penalty, and restart. The goal is to recognize the complete condition set in real time.  
**Dialogue:** Crew chief: “We are applying the published condition, not a familiar habit from another code.”  
**On-screen text:** Status · Location · Timing · Responsibility · Exception  
**Overlays:** Defined status, Player and ball location, Game clock, Shot clock, Primary coverage  
**Decision pause:** What facts must exist before the rule applies? | Which exception could change the result?  
**Ruling reveal:** 

### Scene 3 — 03:00-05:10
**Learning purpose:** Compare legal, illegal, borderline, and disciplined no-action outcomes  
**Location:** Same court with four original play variations  
**Camera:** Four-panel normal-speed comparison with no slow motion initially  
**Action:** Four original examples isolate one decisive fact for Advanced rules and adapted-rules examination: a legal outcome, an illegal outcome, a borderline view, and a correct no-call or no-correction.  
**Narration:** Compare the plays at normal speed. Do not react only to the final result. For Advanced rules and adapted-rules examination, locate the first relevant action, identify legal status before it, and decide whether the evidence completes every required element. A dramatic result does not replace rule evidence.  
**Dialogue:** Partner: “My angle does not complete the missing element. Stay with the ruling unless authorized evidence changes it.”  
**On-screen text:** See the first relevant fact  
**Overlays:** LEGAL, ILLEGAL, BORDERLINE, NO-CALL / NO-CORRECTION  
**Decision pause:** What single fact separates the examples? | Is the available angle complete enough to rule?  
**Ruling reveal:** The ruling follows the complete legal-status and evidence sequence, not appearance, reputation, crowd reaction, or expected outcome.

### Scene 4 — 05:10-07:30
**Learning purpose:** Apply mechanics, coverage, and administration  
**Location:** Court, scorer’s table, and replay station when applicable  
**Camera:** Primary POV, secondary POV, overhead diagram, freeze at status, freeze at decisive fact  
**Action:** The play runs at normal speed, freezes only to verify facts, returns to normal speed, and completes reporting and resumption for Advanced rules and adapted-rules examination.  
**Narration:** Positioning is part of the decision. For Advanced rules and adapted-rules examination, establish the matchup, maintain an open look, observe the complete action, and avoid reaching from a secondary position. After the whistle or administrative decision, confirm player, ball, clock, shot-clock, team-foul, possession, replay, and resumption status.  
**Dialogue:** Calling official: “I have the complete action. Confirm the clock and restart.”  
**On-screen text:** Angle → Evidence → Classification → Administration  
**Overlays:** Primary coverage, Secondary help, Open angle, Clock and shot clock, Penalty and restart  
**Decision pause:** Who owns the play? | What help is factual and permitted? | What is the complete administration?  
**Ruling reveal:** Use the authorized signal, report accurately, restore every required game condition, and resume without unnecessary delay.

### Scene 5 — 07:30-09:10
**Learning purpose:** Demonstrate professional communication and leadership  
**Location:** Court, coach communication lane, crew conference, and postgame reporting room  
**Camera:** Table-facing medium shot, coach-side angle, and crew huddle close-up  
**Action:** The crew communicates the decision for Advanced rules and adapted-rules examination briefly, neutrally, and with source-based accuracy.  
**Narration:** Communication does not replace accuracy, but it protects game flow and trust. State only the information needed for Advanced rules and adapted-rules examination. Do not import terminology from another ruleset, debate indefinitely, criticize a partner, or promise an outcome.  
**Dialogue:** Official: “The ruling is based on the event’s controlling rule and the observed status. We will resume at the designated location with the corrected clock.”  
**On-screen text:** Information, not argument  
**Overlays:** Accurate, Brief, Calm, Neutral, Next action  
**Decision pause:** Who should communicate? | What does the coach need to know now? | What must be documented later?  
**Ruling reveal:** 

### Scene 6 — 09:10-10:45
**Learning purpose:** Complete assessment, reflection, and next-game transfer  
**Location:** RefZone film room and accessible iPad course interface  
**Camera:** iPad close-up with keyboard, caption, transcript, and audio-description controls  
**Action:** The learner answers the decision pause, explains Advanced rules and adapted-rules examination aloud, saves a note, and defines a measurable next-assignment behavior.  
**Narration:** Review Advanced rules and adapted-rules examination at normal speed, use slow motion only to verify facts, and return to normal speed before judging the whistle. Cite the controlling source, explain the decision in original language, administer the entire result, and record one behavior to apply on the next assignment.  
**Dialogue:** Learner: “My next-game cue is to identify the event code and legal status before the decision point.”  
**On-screen text:** Study it. See it. Rule it. Administer it. Review it.  
**Overlays:** Knowledge check, Source citation, Film journal, Next-game cue  
**Decision pause:** Which evidence would change the conclusion? | What will you do differently on the next assignment?  
**Ruling reveal:** Complete the assessment and any assigned remediation before advancing.
