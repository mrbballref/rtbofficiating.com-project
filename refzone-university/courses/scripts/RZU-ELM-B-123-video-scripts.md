## Pivot-foot establishment

**Estimated duration:** 10:45  
**Target audience:** Developing EuroLeague men’s officials  
**Rule/source map:** FIBA Official Basketball Rules — violations and game administration; 2025-26 Euroleague Basketball Officiating Manual — EB casebook and guidelines

### Scene 1 — 00:00-01:20
**Learning purpose:** Frame the authority, effective date, and learning question  
**Location:** RefZone University digital classroom and source-control desk  
**Camera:** Slow push-in to source timeline and course map  
**Action:** The screen identifies Pivot-foot establishment, the applicable event type, and the difference between current-effective, future-effective, and event-specific authority.  
**Narration:** This lesson examines Pivot-foot establishment. Begin by identifying the EuroLeague season, controlling bylaws, adapted rules, officiating manual, technical clarification, and assignment directive. FIBA supplies the underlying playing code, but Euroleague Basketball adaptations and competition procedures control when they differ. Never apply a future-effective FIBA change or a domestic-league procedure without confirmed EuroLeague adoption.  
**Dialogue:** Instructor: “Name the controlling event document before you analyze the play.”  
**On-screen text:** Competition → Source → Effective date → Ruling  
**Overlays:** Euroleague Basketball assignment type, Current-effective source, Future-effective source, Human-review gate  
**Decision pause:** Which competition is being officiated? | Which rule code is expressly adopted? | Is the source current effective or future effective?

### Scene 2 — 01:20-03:00

**Location:** Unbranded regulation basketball court and digital tactics board  
**Camera:** Broadcast sideline moving to overhead tactical view  
**Action:** An original staged sequence illustrates the legal status and governing conditions for Pivot-foot establishment.  
**Narration:** The governing concept is not memorized as isolated wording. The goal is to recognize the complete condition set in real time.  
**Dialogue:** Crew chief: “We are applying the published condition, not a familiar habit from another code.”  
**On-screen text:** Status · Location · Timing · Responsibility · Exception  
**Overlays:** Defined status, Player and ball location, Game clock, Shot clock, Primary coverage

### Scene 3 — 03:00-05:10
**Learning purpose:** Compare legal, illegal, borderline, and disciplined no-action outcomes  
**Location:** Same court with four original play variations  
**Camera:** Four-panel normal-speed comparison with no slow motion initially  
**Action:** Four original examples isolate one decisive fact for Pivot-foot establishment: a legal outcome, an illegal outcome, a borderline view, and a correct no-call or no-correction.  
**Narration:** Compare the plays at normal speed. Do not react only to the final result. For Pivot-foot establishment, locate the first relevant action, identify legal status before it, and decide whether the evidence completes every required element. A dramatic result does not replace rule evidence.

### Scene 4 — 05:10-07:30
**Learning purpose:** Apply mechanics, coverage, and administration

**Camera:** Primary POV, secondary POV, overhead diagram, freeze at status, freeze at decisive fact

- Verify the controlling event rules code and effective date before production.

- Use only original RefZone scenarios, narration, diagrams, assessments, and dialogue.
- Maintain regulation geometry, consistent uniforms, numbers, direction, score, clocks, and possession.

- Use no unlicensed footage, logos, music, broadcasts, real-person likenesses, protected rulebook text, interpretations, or mechanics diagrams.
