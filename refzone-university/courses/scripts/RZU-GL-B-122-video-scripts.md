### Scene 1 · 00:00-01:20
**Learning purpose:** Frame the authority, effective date, and learning question  
**Location/camera:** RefZone University digital classroom and source-control desk · Slow push-in to source timeline and course map  
**Action:** The screen identifies Simulated game preparation and pregame, the applicable event type, and the difference between current-effective, future-effective, and event-specific authority.  
**Narration:** This lesson examines Simulated game preparation and pregame. Begin by identifying the NBA G League season, controlling internal regulations, Official Basketball Rules and authorized competition regulations, officiating manual, official interpretation or technical clarification, and assignment directive. NBA G League supplies the underlying playing code, but NBA G League implementations and competition procedures control when they differ. **Dialogue:** Instructor: “Name the controlling event document before you analyze the play.”

### Scene 2 · 01:20-03:00

**Location/camera:** Unbranded regulation basketball court and digital tactics board · Broadcast sideline moving to overhead tactical view  
**Action:** An original staged sequence illustrates the legal status and governing conditions for Simulated game preparation and pregame.

The goal is to recognize the complete condition set in real time.

**Dialogue:** Crew chief: “We are applying the published condition, not a familiar habit from another code.”

### Scene 3 · 03:00-05:10
**Learning purpose:** Compare legal, illegal, borderline, and disciplined no-action outcomes  
**Location/camera:** Same court with four original play variations · Four-panel normal-speed comparison with no slow motion initially  
**Action:** Four original examples isolate one decisive fact for Simulated game preparation and pregame: a legal outcome, an illegal outcome, a borderline view, and a correct no-call or no-correction.  
**Narration:** Compare the plays at normal speed. Do not react only to the final result. For Simulated game preparation and pregame, locate the first relevant action, identify legal status before it, and decide whether the evidence completes every required element. A dramatic result does not replace rule evidence.  
**On-screen text:** See the first relevant fact  
**Dialogue:** Partner: “My angle does not complete the missing element. Stay with the ruling unless authorized evidence changes it.”  
**Decision pause:** What single fact separates the examples?; Is the available angle complete enough to rule?  
**Ruling reveal:** The ruling follows the complete legal-status and evidence sequence, not appearance, reputation, crowd reaction, or expected outcome.  

### Scene 4 · 05:10-07:30
**Learning purpose:** Apply mechanics, coverage, and administration  
**Location/camera:** Court, scorer’s table, and replay station when applicable · Primary POV, secondary POV, overhead diagram, freeze at status, freeze at decisive fact  
**Action:** The play runs at normal speed, freezes only to verify facts, returns to normal speed, and completes reporting and resumption for Simulated game preparation and pregame.  
**Narration:** Positioning is part of the decision. For Simulated game preparation and pregame, establish the matchup, maintain an open look, observe the complete action, and avoid reaching from a secondary position. After the whistle or administrative decision, confirm player, ball, clock, shot-clock, team-foul, possession, replay, and resumption status.  
**On-screen text:** Angle → Evidence → Classification → Administration  
**Dialogue:** Calling official: “I have the complete action. Confirm the clock and restart.”  
**Decision pause:** Who owns the play?; What help is factual and permitted?; What is the complete administration?  
**Ruling reveal:** Use the authorized signal, report accurately, restore every required game condition, and resume without unnecessary delay.  

### Scene 5 · 07:30-09:10
