## Course Welcome: Becoming a Complete NFHS Official

**Estimated duration:** 6:30  
**Rule references:** RefZone University NFHS Bachelor's-Level program charter; 2026–27 NFHS rules resources; NFHS Learning Center — Officiating Basketball  
**Audience:** NFHS Bachelor's-Level Professional Foundations students  
**Prerequisites:** Course orientation and access to authorized current NFHS sources when assigned  
**Visual style:** Cinematic education-based basketball environment; black, charcoal, metallic silver, white, and RefZone orange; no unlicensed team or league marks  
**Narrator style:** Calm, authoritative, encouraging, precise, and free of theatrical exaggeration  

**Learning objectives**

- Explain course welcome: becoming a complete nfhs official in original professional language.
- Identify the observable behavior expected before, during, and after a contest.
- Apply the professional decision sequence to an original scenario.
- Communicate the response accurately, briefly, calmly, and neutrally.

**Production requirements**

- Actors: Three basketball officials, Two student-athletes, One head coach, One table representative
- Uniforms: Unbranded regulation-style officiating uniforms, Unbranded contrasting team uniforms
- Court: Regulation NFHS basketball court or original accurate simulation
- Equipment: Basketballs, Whistles, Scoreboard and timing display, Table equipment as applicable
- Graphics: RefZone University title card, Key-term cards, Decision sequence, Professional communication captions, Source-reference card
- Sound: Original narration, Natural court ambience, Accessible cue tones

### Scene 1 — 00:00–00:35

**Learning purpose:** Open with the professional problem  
**Location:** Empty high-school gym before warmups  
**Court configuration:** Regulation NFHS court without school logos  
**Players:** None initially; teams enter in the final shot  
**Officials:** Three officials walking from the tunnel to the court  
**Official positions:** Crew moves together, then separates to inspect assigned areas  
**Camera angle:** Wide baseline establishing shot, slow push toward center court  
**Action:** The crew enters a quiet gym as the lesson title appears: Course Welcome: Becoming a Complete NFHS Official.

**Narration:** A whistle is only one part of officiating. In this lesson, we examine course welcome: becoming a complete nfhs official. The goal is not to memorize a slogan. The goal is to build a professional response that can be trusted before, during, and after the contest.

**On-screen text:** Prepare. Observe. Decide. Communicate. Review.

**Visual overlays:** Course code RZU-NFHS-B 101; Course Welcome: Becoming a Complete NFHS Official; Professional Foundations

### Scene 2 — 00:35–01:45

**Learning purpose:** Explain the central concept and intent  
**Location:** Locker-room classroom with tactics board  
**Court configuration:** Half-court diagram on screen  
**Players:** None  
**Officials:** Instructor and two learner officials  
**Official positions:** Seated in an interactive pregame configuration  
**Camera angle:** Medium instructor angle with cutaways to key terms  
**Action:** The instructor uses original RefZone diagrams and a short professional scenario.

**Narration:** This course begins the four-year NFHS Bachelor's-Level Professional Foundations pathway. It develops the habits that make rule knowledge usable: source discipline, ethical judgment, preparation, communication, film review, and accountable reflection. Students need a clear academic and professional operating system before studying hundreds of individual rules and mechanics requirements.

**Dialogue:** Instructor: ‘What responsibility exists even when the play is not in your primary coverage?’ Learner: ‘I remain responsible for the contest and must provide factual help through proper crew procedure.’

**On-screen text:** Responsibility extends beyond the whistle.

**Visual overlays:** What it means; Why it matters; What responsibility requires

### Scene 3 — 01:45–03:15

**Learning purpose:** Show observable professional behavior  
**Location:** Live pregame and first-quarter simulation  
**Court configuration:** Regulation court, scorer's table, team benches, game clock  
**Players:** Two five-player teams in generic uniforms  
**Officials:** Two- or three-person crew as appropriate to the shot  
**Official positions:** Correct pregame and live-ball positions for the situation  
**Camera angle:** Split sequence: broadcast angle, official point of view, table close-up  
**Action:** The video demonstrates the first four observable behaviors in the lesson.

**Narration:** On the floor, professional identity becomes observable behavior. Open the degree map and identify this course's place in Year 1, Term 1. Use the iPad lesson player, transcript, notes, resources, assignments, and video-script tools. Separate official source authority from original RefZone instruction. Complete every required learning activity rather than relying on video completion alone.

**Dialogue:** Official to partner: ‘I have factual information. Let’s conference before we put the ball back in play.’

**On-screen text:** Professionalism is behavior, not a title.

**Visual overlays:** See the whole contest; Use the proper channel; Stay factual; Protect crew unity

### Scene 4 — 03:15–04:35

**Learning purpose:** Decision pause and original case application  
**Location:** Game simulation freezes at the decision moment  
**Court configuration:** Court and table remain visible  
**Players:** Participants hold position during freeze frame  
**Officials:** Calling official, partner, and crew chief visible  
**Official positions:** Primary and secondary coverage highlighted  
**Camera angle:** Normal-speed play, freeze frame, overhead tactical view  
**Action:** A student watches every video but skips source study, case laboratories, and the professional portfolio, then requests course completion.

**Narration:** Pause before the answer. Identify the professional duty. Separate fact from assumption. Determine the proper communication channel. Then select the response that preserves fairness, safety, accurate administration, and trust.

**On-screen text:** DECISION PAUSE

**Visual overlays:** What facts are verified?; Which policy controls?; Who should communicate?; What must be documented?

**Decision pause:** What do you know? | What do you only suspect? | What policy controls? | What is the most professional next action?

### Scene 5 — 04:35–05:45

**Learning purpose:** Reveal the ruling and model communication  
**Location:** Return to live simulation and crew conference  
**Court configuration:** Same court and clock state for continuity  
**Players:** Awaiting correct resumption or administrative decision  
**Officials:** Crew communicates privately, then one official addresses the appropriate person  
**Official positions:** Controlled conference away from benches when practical  
**Camera angle:** Crew close-up followed by coach/table perspective  
**Action:** The crew applies the professional response and resumes the contest correctly.

**Narration:** The correct professional response is: Watching videos alone does not satisfy the five-unit course. The student must complete the required academic, applied, and professional evidence. The communication should be brief and factual.

**On-screen text:** Facts first. Policy second. Professional action next.

**Ruling reveal:** Watching videos alone does not satisfy the five-unit course. The student must complete the required academic, applied, and professional evidence.
