**Course:** Definitions, Court, Equipment, and Game Personnel  
**Program:** NFHS Basketball Officiating Bachelor’s-Level Professional Foundations Program

All narration, scenarios, dialogue, overlays, assessments, diagrams, and production instructions are original RefZone University material. Official publications are reference authorities and are not reproduced.

## Rule 1 Overview: Court Geometry and Required Markings

**Estimated duration:** 7:30  
**Rule references:** NFHS Basketball Rules Book — Rule 1; NFHS Basketball Rules Book — Rule 5; Applicable state-association and assigning-authority policies  
**Audience:** NFHS Bachelor’s-Level Professional Foundations students  
**Prerequisites:** Successful completion of RZU-NFHS-B 102 and all applicable competency gates  
**Visual style:** Cinematic education-based basketball environment using black, charcoal, metallic silver, white, and RefZone orange; original generic court, uniforms, graphics, and diagrams only  
**Narrator style:** Calm, authoritative, precise, evidence-based, and encouraging  

**Learning objectives**

- Explain rule 1 overview: court geometry and required markings in original RefZone University language and cite the controlling source.
- Identify the legal status, timing, location, primary coverage, and visual evidence required before ruling.
- Distinguish a legal example, illegal example, borderline view, and correct no-call or no-correction outcome.
- Apply the correct signal, reporting, penalty, clock, possession, and resumption sequence when applicable.
- Communicate the ruling or professional response accurately, briefly, calmly, and neutrally.
- Review the decision on film and record one measurable next-game application.

**Production requirements**

- Actors: Three basketball officials, Ten student-athlete performers as needed, One head coach, One table representative, One game administrator as needed
- Uniforms: Generic light and dark team uniforms without protected marks, Approved generic officiating uniforms for the depicted mechanics
- Court: Regulation NFHS court geometry with accurate markings and no school, league, or manufacturer marks
- Equipment: Basketball, game clock, score display, whistles, table equipment, authorized safety equipment for the scene
- Graphics: RefZone University title card, rule-reference card, legal-status overlay, primary-coverage shading, decision sequence, clock and game-state card, ruling and resumption card
- Sound: Original or licensed music bed, clean whistle and court sound, clear narration, no broadcast or copyrighted arena audio

### Scene 1 — 00:00–00:40

**Learning purpose:** Establish the lesson question and game context  
**Location:** Generic high-school basketball gym before the contest  
**Court configuration:** Full regulation court with table and clocks visible  
**Players:** Players enter for warmups near the end of the shot  
**Officials:** The assigned officiating crew  
**Official positions:** Crew enters together, then assumes the positions required by the lesson  
**Camera angle:** Wide baseline establishing shot followed by a controlled push to the primary official  
**Action:** The gym transitions from empty preparation to the live context for Rule 1 Overview: Court Geometry and Required Markings.

**Narration:** This lesson examines rule 1 overview: court geometry and required markings. This lesson develops rule 1 overview: court geometry and required markings within Definitions, Court, Equipment, and Game Personnel. Students identify the controlling status, observe the complete action, apply the current NFHS rule or mechanics requirement, administer the result, communicate professionally, and convert the play into a repeatable next-game decision process. The goal is to recognize the controlling facts early enough to make a confident, defensible decision in real time.

**On-screen text:** Establish status before deciding.

**Visual overlays:** RZU-NFHS-B 103; Rule 1 Overview: Court Geometry and Required Markings; NFHS · 2026–27 · Draft pending qualified review

### Scene 2 — 00:40–02:00

**Learning purpose:** Explain the rule, mechanics, or professional concept  
**Location:** RefZone film classroom with original half-court diagram  
**Court configuration:** Interactive diagram and source-reference panel  
**Players:** Animated or staged generic participants  
**Officials:** Instructor and learner officials  
**Official positions:** Positions are labeled for the active rules or mechanics concept  
**Camera angle:** Medium instructor view with overhead diagram inserts  
**Action:** The instructor builds the concept from legal status through administration using original diagrams.

**Narration:** Court, equipment, personnel, and duty rules create the environment in which every later judgment occurs. Preventive inspection and correct administrative responsibility reduce avoidable disruptions and safety risks. For this lesson, focus on these observable elements: Confirm the court, equipment, participant, and official status before the contest begins. Inspect the required location, dimensions, markings, equipment, and personnel role. Identify which official or game administrator owns the correction. Resolve or report a deficiency before it becomes a live-ball problem.

**Dialogue:** Instructor: ‘What fact must be established before the action?’ Learner: ‘The legal status that controls the entire sequence.’

**On-screen text:** Rule meaning → visual evidence → decision → administration

**Visual overlays:** What it means; Why it matters; What the official must observe

### Scene 3 — 02:00–03:25

**Learning purpose:** Demonstrate legal, illegal, borderline, and correct no-call examples  
**Location:** Live staged game sequence  
**Court configuration:** Full or half court selected for the play  
**Players:** Generic players repeat four controlled versions of the action  
**Officials:** Correct crew size for the depicted system  
**Official positions:** Primary and secondary officials are highlighted  
**Camera angle:** Normal-speed broadcast view, primary official point of view, and overhead tactical view  
**Action:** Four original versions of the play are performed with identical starting alignment and different legal facts.

**Narration:** The legal example preserves the required status and creates no prohibited effect. The illegal example contains the specific missing or prohibited element. The borderline example does not provide enough evidence to guess. The correct no-call protects legal play and game flow. Confirm the correct mechanics, communication, and administration.

**Dialogue:** Crew partner: ‘I have factual help if you need it.’ Calling official: ‘Hold it for the conference; I want the complete sequence.’

**On-screen text:** Do not decide from the result alone.

**Visual overlays:** LEGAL; ILLEGAL; BORDERLINE — IMPROVE THE ANGLE; CORRECT NO-CALL

### Scene 4 — 03:25–05:00

**Learning purpose:** Decision pause using an original case play  
**Location:** Staged game freezes at the critical moment  
**Court configuration:** Clock, score, possession, and crew positions remain visible  
**Players:** Participants hold the contact, movement, or administration position  
**Officials:** Primary, secondary, and crew chief are visible  
**Official positions:** Primary coverage is shaded; alternative angles are displayed after the pause  
**Camera angle:** Normal-speed live angle, primary point of view, freeze frame, and overhead diagram  
**Action:** An official must apply rule 1 overview: court geometry and required markings during a close game while partners, coaches, and table personnel have incomplete information.

**Narration:** Pause before the ruling. Identify the required court, equipment, participant, or official status. Inspect the condition before the contest or at the first safe opportunity. Determine whether the condition is legal, correctable, or unsafe. Work through the complete sequence before reacting to the final result.

**On-screen text:** DECISION PAUSE

**Visual overlays:** What is the legal status?; What facts are verified?; Who owns the play?; What is the penalty or resumption?

**Decision pause:** What happened first? | Which rule or mechanics requirement controls? | What evidence is missing? | What is the correct ruling, penalty, and resumption?

### Scene 5 — 05:00–06:35

**Learning purpose:** Reveal the ruling, mechanics, reporting, and communication  
**Location:** Same court and game state for continuity  
**Court configuration:** Table and benches visible for administration  
**Players:** Players wait in the correct locations for resumption  
**Officials:** Crew conferences, reports, and resumes  
**Official positions:** Correct reporting and resumption positions  
**Camera angle:** Crew close-up, table perspective, coach perspective, and restart wide shot  
**Action:** The crew applies the final ruling and demonstrates the complete restart.

**Narration:** The correct response is: Use the controlling source, observe the complete facts, apply the required decision sequence, communicate briefly, and resume correctly. Complete the administration before the next live ball, then communicate only what is necessary.

**Dialogue:** Official: ‘We have confirmed the facts and the applicable rule. This is the final ruling and resumption.’

**On-screen text:** Ruling → penalty → clock → possession → resumption

**Visual overlays:** Correct ruling; Penalty; Clock and possession; Signal and communication

**Ruling reveal:** Use the controlling source, observe the complete facts, apply the required decision sequence, communicate briefly, and resume correctly.

### Scene 6 — 06:35–07:30

**Learning purpose:** Knowledge check and next-game application  
**Location:** Postgame film room  
**Court configuration:** Normal-speed replay and learner journal on screen  
**Players:** None  
**Officials:** Learner and instructor  
**Official positions:** Not applicable  
**Camera angle:** Over-shoulder journal shot followed by direct-to-camera close  
**Action:** The learner records the controlling source, one corrected misconception, and one measurable next-game behavior.

**Narration:** Mastery requires more than recognizing the answer. Explain rule 1 overview: court geometry and required markings in original language, complete the lesson assignment, practice the communication aloud, and record the specific behavior you will apply in the next contest.

**Dialogue:** Learner: ‘My next-game objective is specific, observable, and connected to the rule and mechanics evidence.’

**On-screen text:** What will you see earlier next time?

**Visual overlays:** Review at normal speed; Record the evidence; Apply in the next game; Sources: NFHS Basketball Rules Book — Rule 1; NFHS Basketball Rules Book — Rule 5

**Accessibility and delivery**

- Captions: Create approved WebVTT and SRT caption files from the final narration and dialogue.
- Transcript: Publish a searchable transcript with scene timestamps and source-reference labels.
- Audio description: Describe court location, player movement, official movement, signals, freeze frames, diagrams, and all overlays not conveyed in narration.
- Thumbnail: RefZone University iPad frame, generic high-school basketball action illustrating Rule 1 Overview: Court Geometry and Required Markings, title set in accessible high-contrast text, no protected marks.

**Quality-control checklist**

- Confirm all narration, scenarios, dialogue, diagrams, and assessments are original RefZone University material.
- Validate every rule, penalty, signal, mechanics position, and current-season update with qualified NFHS reviewers.
- Use regulation court geometry, consistent players, uniforms, direction, score, clock, and ball movement.
- Reject distorted hands, bodies, basketballs, court lines, signals, or impossible movement.
- Use no unlicensed game footage, logos, music, broadcasts, real-person likenesses, or protected publication text and diagrams.
- Provide captions, searchable transcript, audio description, keyboard controls, reduced-motion alternatives, and high-contrast graphics.
