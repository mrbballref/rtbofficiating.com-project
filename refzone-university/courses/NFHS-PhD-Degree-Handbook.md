# NFHS PhD-Level Research, Leadership, and Elite Officiating Program Handbook

**Recommended sequence:** 4 years · 8 academic terms  
**Program volume:** 20 courses · 60 RefZone doctoral credit-equivalent units · 900 engagement hours  
**Credential status:** Professional doctoral degree-level pathway; not an accredited or transferable academic degree  
**Required milestones:** Doctoral coursework · candidacy examination · dissertation proposal defense · applied dissertation or institutional research project · final oral defense · professional contribution portfolio  

## Program Mission

Develop elite officials and institutional leaders who can conduct rigorous research, analyze policy, improve evaluation and curriculum, govern technology responsibly, lead organizations ethically, and make original contributions that strengthen basketball officiating.

## Admission Standard

- Completed NFHS Master’s-Level Advanced Officiating pathway or verified equivalent competency.
- Elite rules, mechanics, judgment, game-management, leadership, evaluation, and film readiness.
- Demonstrated scholarly writing and research-methods readiness.
- Verified ethics, integrity, confidentiality, and professional-conduct standing.
- Approved research interest or institutional problem area.

## Doctoral Milestone Gates

1. Coursework gate: all required courses through publication and policy research completed.
2. Candidacy gate: written, applied, and oral examinations passed.
3. Proposal gate: significant problem, literature, framework, method, ethics, feasibility, and contribution approved.
4. Research gate: approved protocol, secure data, auditable analysis, and committee monitoring maintained.
5. Defense gate: final manuscript, oral defense, revisions, dissemination plan, and professional portfolio approved.

## Academic Integrity and Publication

- All official rules and mechanics remain source-controlled and current-effective.
- All research, legal, policy, technology, and equity claims require validated support.
- All AI assistance must be disclosed, reviewed, and subordinate to accountable human judgment.
- No protected publication, footage, likeness, confidential record, or identifiable participant data may be used without authorization.
- No course or research product is represented as approved, accredited, publishable, or generalizable beyond its evidence and review status.
