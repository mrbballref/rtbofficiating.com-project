# RZU-NCAAD1W-D 701 — Doctoral Foundations in Officiating Scholarship

**Program:** NCAA Division I Women’s Basketball Officiating PhD-Level Research, Leadership, and Elite Officiating Pathway  
**Academic placement:** Year 1, Term 1  
**Units:** 3 RefZone doctoral credit-equivalent units  
**Engagement:** 45 hours  
**Prerequisite:** Completed NCAA Division I Women’s Master’s-Level Advanced Officiating pathway or approved equivalent elite officiating, leadership, evaluation, and research-readiness verification  
**Credential status:** Professional doctoral degree-level pathway; not an accredited or transferable academic degree  
**Publication status:** Complete instructional production draft; required human review remains pending  

## Course Description

A three-unit doctoral course in the NCAA Division I Women’s PhD-Level Research, Leadership, and Elite Officiating pathway focused on doctoral foundations in officiating scholarship. The course integrates current NCAA women’s playing-rules authority and NCAA Division I administrative authority, doctoral scholarship, original research or policy work, ethics, equity, professional application, peer review, and oral defense.

## Learning Outcomes

- Critically synthesize the doctoral domain of doctoral foundations in officiating scholarship using current NCAA women’s playing-rules authority and NCAA Division I administrative authority and validated research evidence.
- Design and defend an appropriate research, policy, analytic, leadership, curriculum, technology, or evaluation method.
- Evaluate alternative explanations, uncertainty, ethics, equity, privacy, copyright, governance, and implementation risk.
- Produce original doctoral writing, evidence visualizations, peer review, revision, and oral defense.
- Translate findings into a responsible professional or institutional contribution.
- Maintain complete source versioning, research records, human-review gates, and academic integrity.

## Source and Copyright Controls

- Official NCAA women’s and NCAA Division I publications and current official updates are reference authorities; student-facing instruction is original RefZone University language.
- No protected rulebook text, casebook language, mechanics diagrams, game footage, logos, or identifiable participant data may be reproduced without authorization.
- Research literature, legal and policy sources, technology claims, and institutional procedures require source-by-source validation.
- Any uncertainty, source conflict, inaccessible authority, ethics concern, or jurisdiction-specific requirement remains blocked for human review.
