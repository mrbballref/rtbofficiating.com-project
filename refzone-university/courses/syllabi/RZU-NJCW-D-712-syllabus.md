## Course Description

A three-unit doctoral course in the NJCAA Women’s PhD-Level Research, Leadership, and Elite Officiating pathway focused on attention, anticipation, pattern recognition, and cognitive load. The course integrates current NCAA women’s playing-rules authority and NJCAA administrative authority, doctoral scholarship, original research or policy work, ethics, equity, professional application, peer review, and oral defense.

## Learning Outcomes

- Critically synthesize the doctoral domain of attention, anticipation, pattern recognition, and cognitive load using current NCAA women’s playing-rules authority and NJCAA administrative authority and validated research evidence.
- Design and defend an appropriate research, policy, analytic, leadership, curriculum, technology, or evaluation method.
- Evaluate alternative explanations, uncertainty, ethics, equity, privacy, copyright, governance, and implementation risk.
- Produce original doctoral writing, evidence visualizations, peer review, revision, and oral defense.
- Translate findings into a responsible professional or institutional contribution.
- Maintain complete source versioning, research records, human-review gates, and academic integrity.

1. Selective Attention and Matchup Ownership
2. Sustained Attention Across the Complete Contest
3. Divided Attention: Ball, Off-Ball, Clock, and Crew
4. Inattentional Blindness and Change Blindness
5. Anticipation Without Guessing
6. Pattern Recognition in Screening and Offensive Actions
7. Expert Cue Utilization and Pre-Contact Information
8. Cognitive Load During Press, Transition, and End Game
9. Working Memory and Penalty Sequencing
10. Fatigue, Stress, Noise, and Attentional Control
11. Crew Distribution as a Cognitive System
12. Pregame Planning and Mental Schema Activation
13. Attention Training and Deliberate Practice Design
14. Pattern-Recognition Calibration Laboratory
15. Cognitive-Load Research Proposal
