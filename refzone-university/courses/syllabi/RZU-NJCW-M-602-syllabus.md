# RZU-NJCW-M 602 — Crew-Chief Leadership and Crew Performance

**Program:** NJCAA Women’s Basketball Officiating Master’s-Level Advanced Officiating Program  
**Academic period:** Year 2 · Term 3  
**Term theme:** Leadership, Evaluation, Communication, and Film Analytics  
**Learning volume:** 3 RefZone graduate credit-equivalent units · 45 engagement hours  
**Prerequisite:** RZU-NJCW-M 601  
**Credential status:** Professional graduate degree-level pathway; not an accredited degree or transferable graduate credit  
**Rules basis:** Current NCAA women’s playing rules, approved rulings, interpretations, replay and challenge guidance, authorized mechanics, 2026-27 approved rulings and points of emphasis, and verified NJCAA procedures  

A graduate-level NJCAA women’s basketball officiating course in crew-chief leadership and crew performance, integrating current NCAA women’s playing rules, approved rulings, NJCAA procedures, advanced mechanics, normal-speed film analysis, written rules work, practical performance, and oral defense.

## Graduate learning outcomes

- Analyze crew-chief leadership and crew performance with current NCAA women’s and NJCAA authority.
- Resolve advanced legal-status, mechanics, administration, and communication problems without cross-ruleset contamination.
- Defend conclusions through original written analysis, normal-speed film, case-play work, and oral examination.
- Translate graduate study into measurable crew, evaluator, leadership, or game-performance behavior.

## Fifteen-week sequence

- **Week 1:** Crew Chief as Facilitator, Not Dominator
- **Week 2:** Assignment Acceptance, Conflict Review, and Partner Contact
- **Week 3:** Travel, Arrival, Site Verification, and Contingency Planning
- **Week 4:** Building an Interactive NCAA Women’s and NJCAA-Specific Pregame
- **Week 5:** Court, Equipment, Table, Replay, Security, and Medical Readiness
- **Week 6:** Setting Crew Expectations Without Undermining Partners
- **Week 7:** Primary Coverage, Help Philosophy, and Double-Whistle Leadership
- **Week 8:** Clock, Shot Clock, Media Timeout, and Challenge Readiness
- **Week 9:** Leading Replay and Complex-Administration Conferences
- **Week 10:** Supporting a Partner After a Difficult Play
- **Week 11:** Correcting Factual Errors Without Embarrassing the Crew
- **Week 12:** Managing Injury, Fighting, Security, and Suspended-Game Events
- **Week 13:** Postgame Review, Confidentiality, and Supervisor Communication
- **Week 14:** Crew Performance Plan and Partner Development
- **Week 15:** Graduate Crew-Chief Leadership Evaluation

## Required graduate submissions

- Current-source graduate memorandum
- Original legal, illegal, borderline, and correct no-call or no-correction case set
- Normal-speed film analysis with angle limitations
- Mechanics, replay, communication, evaluation, or leadership artifact appropriate to the course
- Applied performance goal and verification evidence
- Final oral defense and remediation record

## Grading

- Graduate source memoranda and case analysis: 20%
- Film, mechanics, replay, or communication laboratories: 20%
- Applied performance and professional artifacts: 25%
- Midterm and comprehensive examination: 20%
- Final portfolio and oral defense: 15%

## Completion standard

Minimum 85 percent overall, completion of every mandatory competency gate, and successful oral defense or re-demonstration.

## Lessons

### 1. Crew Chief as Facilitator, Not Dominator

This graduate lesson develops crew chief as facilitator, not dominator within Crew-Chief Leadership and Crew Performance. The learner integrates current NCAA women’s playing rules, approved rulings, applicable NJCAA procedures, advanced mechanics, normal-speed film evidence, complete administration, professional communication, and measurable applied performance.

**Objectives**

- Analyze crew chief as facilitator, not dominator using current NCAA women’s and applicable NJCAA authority.
- Identify operative legal status, game status, crew responsibility, and the exact evidence required for a defensible decision.
- Distinguish supported, unsupported, borderline, and disciplined no-action outcomes.
- Apply the complete penalty, clock, shot-clock, timeout, challenge, possession, reporting, and resumption sequence when applicable.
- Defend the conclusion, mechanics, communication, and source choice through original writing, film review, applied evidence, and oral examination.
- Translate the lesson into a measurable next-game, crew-leadership, evaluation, or instructional behavior.

**Floor and evidence checklist**

- Assignment status
- Partner readiness
- Travel and arrival
- Rules and procedure updates
- Coverage plan
- Table and replay readiness
- Emergency plan
- Postgame and reporting responsibility

**Decision sequence**

- Verify assignment and policy.
- Contact partners and establish a clear plan.
- Invite partner contribution to the pregame.
- Confirm site, table, replay, security, and emergency readiness.
- Set coverage and communication expectations.
- Lead difficult conferences with facts and humility.
- Support partners and accept responsibility.
- Conduct postgame review and complete reports.

**Original graduate scenario**

- Setup: Before and during “Crew Chief as Facilitator, Not Dominator,” the crew faces incomplete partner communication, a facility issue, a replay uncertainty, and a difficult double whistle.
- Conclusion: Verify policy, create a collaborative plan, resolve site readiness, clarify coverage, lead the conference with facts, support the partner, complete administration, and document the postgame follow-up.
- Communication: Let us confirm what each official saw, identify the primary responsibility, apply the current rule, and present one clear crew ruling.

**Study and assessment work**

- Read the assigned current source and record version, effective date, definitions, conditions, exceptions, penalty, reviewability, and related mechanics without copying protected wording.
- Write a 500–750 word graduate memorandum on crew chief as facilitator, not dominator with a source map and a clearly labeled RefZone instructional-analysis section.
- Create an original four-example comparison: supported, unsupported, borderline, and disciplined no-action.
- Tag two film or simulation clips at normal speed, identify angle limitations, and defend the ruling and mechanics with current authority.
- Record a three-minute oral defense and a ten-second floor, coach, table, crew, evaluator, or supervisor communication.
- Submit one measurable applied-performance goal and the evidence that will verify improvement.
