# RZU-NAIM-M 522 — Replay, Coach’s Challenges, Appeals, and Atypical Plays

**Program:** NAIA Men’s Basketball Officiating Master’s-Level Advanced Officiating Program  
**Academic period:** Year 1 · Term 2  
**Term theme:** Advanced Mechanics, Timing, Replay, and Game Administration  
**Learning volume:** 3 RefZone graduate credit-equivalent units · 45 engagement hours  
**Prerequisite:** RZU-NAIM-M 521  
**Credential status:** Professional graduate degree-level pathway; not an accredited degree or transferable graduate credit  
**Rules basis:** Current NCAA men’s playing rules for the 2025-26 and 2026-27 cycle, approved rulings, interpretations, replay and challenge guidance, authorized mechanics, and verified NAIA men’s basketball modifications and administrative procedures  

A graduate-level NAIA men’s basketball officiating course in replay, coach’s challenges, appeals, and atypical plays, integrating current NCAA men’s playing rules, approved rulings, NAIA procedures, advanced mechanics, normal-speed film analysis, written rules work, practical performance, and oral defense.

## Graduate learning outcomes

- Analyze replay, coach’s challenges, appeals, and atypical plays with current NCAA men’s and NAIA authority.
- Resolve advanced legal-status, mechanics, administration, and communication problems without cross-ruleset contamination.
- Defend conclusions through original written analysis, normal-speed film, case-play work, and oral examination.
- Translate graduate study into measurable crew, evaluator, leadership, or game-performance behavior.

## Fifteen-week sequence

- **Week 1:** NCAA Men’s Replay Philosophy and the Ruling on the Floor
- **Week 2:** Reviewable and Non-Reviewable Elements
- **Week 3:** Evidence Standards and Indisputable Evidence
- **Week 4:** Coach’s Challenge Eligibility, Timing, Failure Penalty, and Review Scope
- **Week 5:** Called Out-of-Bounds Challenges and Permitted Review Scope
- **Week 6:** Called Goaltending and Basket-Interference Challenges
- **Week 7:** Called Restricted-Area and Block-Charge Challenge Administration
- **Week 8:** Flagrant, Fighting, and Hostile-Act Review
- **Week 9:** Clock, Shot-Clock, Two-Point, and Three-Point Corrections
- **Week 10:** Replay Crew Conference, Table Communication, and Resumption
- **Week 11:** Monitor Efficiency and Avoiding Unauthorized Review Expansion
- **Week 12:** Replay-System Failure and No-Replay Contingency
- **Week 13:** Correctable Errors, Wrong Shooter, and Incorrect Award
- **Week 14:** Atypical Plays Laboratory: Chronology to Resumption
- **Week 15:** Replay and Rare-Play Comprehensive Examination

## Required graduate submissions

- Current-source graduate memorandum
- Original legal, illegal, borderline, and correct no-call or no-correction case set
- Normal-speed film analysis with angle limitations
- Mechanics, replay, communication, evaluation, or leadership artifact appropriate to the course
- Applied performance goal and verification evidence
- Final oral defense and remediation record

## Grading

- Graduate source memoranda and case analysis: 20%
- Film, mechanics, replay, or communication laboratories: 20%
- Applied performance and professional artifacts: 25%
- Midterm and comprehensive examination: 20%
- Final portfolio and oral defense: 15%

## Completion standard

Minimum 85 percent overall, completion of every mandatory competency gate, and successful oral defense or re-demonstration.

## Lessons

### 1. NCAA Men’s Replay Philosophy and the Ruling on the Floor

This graduate lesson develops ncaa men’s replay philosophy and the ruling on the floor within Replay, Coach’s Challenges, Appeals, and Atypical Plays. The learner integrates current NCAA men’s playing rules, approved rulings, applicable NAIA procedures, advanced mechanics, normal-speed film evidence, complete administration, professional communication, and measurable applied performance.

**Objectives**

- Analyze ncaa men’s replay philosophy and the ruling on the floor using current NCAA men’s and applicable NAIA authority.
- Identify operative legal status, game status, crew responsibility, and the exact evidence required for a defensible decision.
- Distinguish supported, unsupported, borderline, and disciplined no-action outcomes.
- Apply the complete penalty, clock, shot-clock, timeout, challenge, possession, reporting, and resumption sequence when applicable.
- Defend the conclusion, mechanics, communication, and source choice through original writing, film review, applied evidence, and oral examination.
- Translate the lesson into a measurable next-game, crew-leadership, evaluation, or instructional behavior.

**Floor and evidence checklist**

- Ruling on the floor
- Review question
- Permitted review element
- Challenge prerequisites
- Available angles
- Evidence standard
- Clock and shot-clock result
- Final announcement and resumption

**Decision sequence**

- State the ruling on the floor.
- Define the one permitted review question.
- Verify challenge or crew-initiated prerequisites.
- Identify the available review elements and evidence standard.
- Review efficiently without searching for unauthorized infractions.
- Decide confirm, reverse, or no-change.
- Correct permitted clock, shot-clock, foul, score, and possession elements.
- Announce and resume the game without unnecessary delay.

**Original graduate scenario**

- Setup: A coach requests review during “NCAA Men’s Replay Philosophy and the Ruling on the Floor.” The crew must decide whether the request is timely, whether the play is challengeable, what elements may be reviewed, and whether the evidence meets the required standard.
- Conclusion: State the ruling on the floor, verify challenge prerequisites, define the review question, examine only authorized elements, apply the evidence standard, announce the outcome, adjust permitted game-state elements, and resume efficiently.
- Communication: The challenge is permitted for this called play. The ruling on the floor is being reviewed only for the authorized element; the crew will announce the result and the exact resumption.

**Study and assessment work**

- Read the assigned current source and record version, effective date, definitions, conditions, exceptions, penalty, reviewability, and related mechanics without copying protected wording.
- Write a 500–750 word graduate memorandum on ncaa men’s replay philosophy and the ruling on the floor with a source map and a clearly labeled RefZone instructional-analysis section.
- Create an original four-example comparison: supported, unsupported, borderline, and disciplined no-action.
- Tag two film or simulation clips at normal speed, identify angle limitations, and defend the ruling and mechanics with current authority.
- Record a three-minute oral defense and a ten-second floor, coach, table, crew, evaluator, or supervisor communication.
- Submit one measurable applied-performance goal and the evidence that will verify improvement.
