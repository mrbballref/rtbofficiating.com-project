# RZU-NAIAW-M 521 — Transition, Press, End-of-Game, Clock, and Shot-Clock Administration

**Program:** NAIA Women’s Basketball Officiating Master’s-Level Advanced Officiating Program  
**Academic period:** Year 1 · Term 2  
**Term theme:** Advanced Mechanics, Timing, Replay, and Game Administration  
**Learning volume:** 3 RefZone graduate credit-equivalent units · 45 engagement hours  
**Prerequisite:** RZU-NAIAW-M 512  
**Credential status:** Professional graduate degree-level pathway; not an accredited degree or transferable graduate credit  
**Rules basis:** Current NCAA women’s playing rules for the 2025-26 and 2026-27 cycle, approved rulings, interpretations, replay and challenge guidance, authorized mechanics, and verified NAIA women’s basketball modifications and administrative procedures  

A graduate-level NAIA women’s basketball officiating course in transition, press, end-of-game, clock, and shot-clock administration, integrating current NCAA women’s playing rules, approved rulings, NAIA procedures, advanced mechanics, normal-speed film analysis, written rules work, practical performance, and oral defense.

## Graduate learning outcomes

- Analyze transition, press, end-of-game, clock, and shot-clock administration with current NCAA women’s and NAIA authority.
- Resolve advanced legal-status, mechanics, administration, and communication problems without cross-ruleset contamination.
- Defend conclusions through original written analysis, normal-speed film, case-play work, and oral examination.
- Translate graduate study into measurable crew, evaluator, leadership, or game-performance behavior.

## Fifteen-week sequence

- **Week 1:** Transition Phases and Crew Conversion
- **Week 2:** Backcourt Press Coverage and the Ten-Second Count
- **Week 3:** The 20-Second Frontcourt Reset and Shot-Clock Display Administration
- **Week 4:** Traps, Run-and-Jump Actions, and Multiple Matchups
- **Week 5:** Long Passes, Deep Receivers, and Boundary Coverage
- **Week 6:** Fast Breaks, Secondary Defenders, and Late Arrivals
- **Week 7:** Last-Shot Responsibility and End-of-Period Crew Planning
- **Week 8:** Try, Tap, Release, Horn, and Period-End Status
- **Week 9:** Game-Clock Starts, Stops, Delays, and Malfunctions
- **Week 10:** Shot-Clock Starts, Stops, Resets, and Full or Partial Resets
- **Week 11:** Definite Information and Clock or Shot-Clock Corrections
- **Week 12:** Table-Crew Communication, Shot-Clock Resets, and Malfunction Procedures
- **Week 13:** Quarter Breaks, Media Timeouts, Intermissions, and Resumption
- **Week 14:** Late-Game Timeout, Substitution, Advance, and Throw-In Administration
- **Week 15:** End-of-Game Administration Simulation and Oral Defense

## Required graduate submissions

- Current-source graduate memorandum
- Original legal, illegal, borderline, and correct no-call or no-correction case set
- Normal-speed film analysis with angle limitations
- Mechanics, replay, communication, evaluation, or leadership artifact appropriate to the course
- Applied performance goal and verification evidence
- Final oral defense and remediation record

## Grading

- Graduate source memoranda and case analysis: 20%
- Film, mechanics, replay, or communication laboratories: 20%
- Applied performance and professional artifacts: 25%
- Midterm and comprehensive examination: 20%
- Final portfolio and oral defense: 15%

## Completion standard

Minimum 85 percent overall, completion of every mandatory competency gate, and successful oral defense or re-demonstration.

## Lessons

### 1. Transition Phases and Crew Conversion

This graduate lesson develops transition phases and crew conversion within Transition, Press, End-of-Game, Clock, and Shot-Clock Administration. The learner integrates current NCAA women’s playing rules, approved rulings, applicable NAIA procedures, advanced mechanics, normal-speed film evidence, complete administration, professional communication, and measurable applied performance.

**Objectives**

- Analyze transition phases and crew conversion using current NCAA women’s and applicable NAIA authority.
- Identify operative legal status, game status, crew responsibility, and the exact evidence required for a defensible decision.
- Distinguish supported, unsupported, borderline, and disciplined no-action outcomes.
- Apply the complete penalty, clock, shot-clock, timeout, challenge, possession, reporting, and resumption sequence when applicable.
- Defend the conclusion, mechanics, communication, and source choice through original writing, film review, applied evidence, and oral examination.
- Translate the lesson into a measurable next-game, crew-leadership, evaluation, or instructional behavior.

**Floor and evidence checklist**

- Game clock
- Shot-clock display
- Possession and control
- Count start and termination
- Try or tap status
- Timeout and media status
- Definite information
- Correct restart and table confirmation

**Decision sequence**

- Identify game and shot-clock status before the action.
- Know the official and table responsibilities.
- Observe the start, stop, reset, horn, timeout, or count fact.
- Determine whether definite information exists.
- Apply current timing and media provisions.
- Correct only what the rules permit.
- Communicate the exact clock and restart to the table and coaches.
- Verify displays before making the ball live.

**Original graduate scenario**

- Setup: In a late-game situation involving “Transition Phases and Crew Conversion,” the horn, game clock, shot-clock display, timeout status, and table information do not initially align.
- Conclusion: Determine the status before the event, identify definite information, apply the current NCAA women’s timing rule and NAIA procedure, correct only permitted elements, communicate the exact displays, and verify before resumption.
- Communication: We have definite information for the permitted correction. The game clock will be set to the announced time, the shot clock to the announced value, and play will resume from the stated location.

**Study and assessment work**

- Read the assigned current source and record version, effective date, definitions, conditions, exceptions, penalty, reviewability, and related mechanics without copying protected wording.
- Write a 500–750 word graduate memorandum on transition phases and crew conversion with a source map and a clearly labeled RefZone instructional-analysis section.
- Create an original four-example comparison: supported, unsupported, borderline, and disciplined no-action.
- Tag two film or simulation clips at normal speed, identify angle limitations, and defend the ruling and mechanics with current authority.
- Record a three-minute oral defense and a ten-second floor, coach, table, crew, evaluator, or supervisor communication.
- Submit one measurable applied-performance goal and the evidence that will verify improvement.
