# RZU-D1W-M 502 — Complex Case Plays, Penalty Sequencing, and Correctable Errors

**Program:** NCAA Division I Women’s Basketball Officiating Master’s-Level Advanced Officiating Program  
**Academic period:** Year 1 · Term 1  
**Term theme:** Advanced Rules Interpretation, Complex Cases, and Judgment  
**Learning volume:** 3 RefZone graduate credit-equivalent units · 45 engagement hours  
**Prerequisite:** RZU-D1W-M 501  
**Credential status:** Professional graduate degree-level pathway; not an accredited degree or transferable graduate credit  
**Rules basis:** Current NCAA women’s playing rules for the 2025-26 and 2026-27 cycle, approved rulings, interpretations, replay and challenge guidance, authorized mechanics, and verified NCAA Division I Women’s basketball modifications and administrative procedures  

A graduate-level NCAA Division I Women’s basketball officiating course in complex case plays, penalty sequencing, and correctable errors, integrating current NCAA women’s playing rules, approved rulings, NCAA Division I conference and championship procedures, advanced mechanics, normal-speed film analysis, written rules work, practical performance, and oral defense.

## Graduate learning outcomes

- Analyze complex case plays, penalty sequencing, and correctable errors with current NCAA women’s playing-rules and Division I administrative authority.
- Resolve advanced legal-status, mechanics, administration, and communication problems without cross-ruleset contamination.
- Defend conclusions through original written analysis, normal-speed film, case-play work, and oral examination.
- Translate graduate study into measurable crew, evaluator, leadership, or game-performance behavior.

## Fifteen-week sequence

- **Week 1:** Chronological Reconstruction of Multi-Event Collegiate Plays
- **Week 2:** Ball Status, Control, Try Status, and Clock Status in Complex Plays
- **Week 3:** Multiple Fouls and Independent Penalty Administration
- **Week 4:** Double Fouls, Simultaneous Fouls, and Point-of-Interruption
- **Week 5:** False Double Fouls and Intervening Live-Ball Action
- **Week 6:** Team, Player, Bench, and Dead-Ball Technical Fouls in a Sequence
- **Week 7:** Flagrant 1, Flagrant 2, Fighting, and Ejection Sequencing
- **Week 8:** Bench Personnel, Leaving the Bench, and Fighting Suspensions
- **Week 9:** Correctable Errors and Time-Limited Correction Windows
- **Week 10:** Wrong Shooter, Wrong Basket, and Incorrect Free-Throw Awards
- **Week 11:** Inadvertent Whistles, Interrupted Plays, and Resumption
- **Week 12:** Book, Score, Clock, Shot-Clock, and Table Discrepancies
- **Week 13:** End-of-Period, Media Timeout, and Overtime Multi-Event Cases
- **Week 14:** Rare-Play Crew Conference and Final Ruling Presentation
- **Week 15:** Complex Case-Play Written Examination and Oral Administration Defense

## Required graduate submissions

- Current-source graduate memorandum
- Original legal, illegal, borderline, and correct no-call or no-correction case set
- Normal-speed film analysis with angle limitations
- Mechanics, replay, communication, evaluation, or leadership artifact appropriate to the course
- Applied performance goal and verification evidence
- Final oral defense and remediation record

## Grading

- Graduate source memoranda and case analysis: 20%
- Film, mechanics, replay, or communication laboratories: 20%
- Applied performance and professional artifacts: 25%
- Midterm and comprehensive examination: 20%
- Final portfolio and oral defense: 15%

## Completion standard

Minimum 85 percent overall, completion of every mandatory competency gate, and successful oral defense or re-demonstration.

### 1. Chronological Reconstruction of Multi-Event Collegiate Plays

This graduate lesson develops chronological reconstruction of multi-event collegiate plays within Complex Case Plays, Penalty Sequencing, and Correctable Errors. The learner integrates current NCAA women’s playing rules, approved rulings, applicable NCAA Division I conference and championship procedures, advanced mechanics, normal-speed film evidence, complete administration, professional communication, and measurable applied performance.

**Objectives**

- Analyze chronological reconstruction of multi-event collegiate plays using current NCAA women’s playing-rules and applicable Division I administrative authority.
- Identify operative legal status, game status, crew responsibility, and the exact evidence required for a defensible decision.
- Distinguish supported, unsupported, borderline, and disciplined no-action outcomes.
- Apply the complete penalty, clock, shot-clock, timeout, challenge, possession, reporting, and resumption sequence when applicable.
- Defend the conclusion, mechanics, communication, and source choice through original writing, film review, applied evidence, and oral examination.
- Translate the lesson into a measurable next-game, crew-leadership, evaluation, or instructional behavior.

**Floor and evidence checklist**

- Chronological order
- Ball status
- Player and team control
- Try and shooter status
- Foul or violation classifications
- Reviewability
- Penalty cancellation or retention
- Clock, shot clock, possession, and resumption

**Decision sequence**

- Freeze the chronology without skipping intervening action.
- Identify ball, player-control, team-control, try, and clock status at each event.
- Classify each foul, violation, technical, or administrative error independently.
- Determine which penalties cancel and which remain.
- Apply correctable-error and replay limits.
- Administer free throws and possession in the proper order.
- Correct game and shot clocks using permitted information.
- Report and resume from the correct location.

**Original graduate scenario**

- Setup: During “Chronological Reconstruction of Multi-Event Collegiate Plays,” a live-ball event, a dead-ball act, a clock issue, and a table error occur in close succession. The crew initially focuses on the final event and overlooks an earlier status change.
- Conclusion: Reconstruct the play chronologically, classify every event, determine replay and correctable-error limits, administer remaining penalties in order, correct permitted timing elements, and resume at the proper location.
- Communication: We have multiple events. The crew is sequencing them in order, confirming the clock and shot clock, and will report the complete administration before resuming.

**Study and assessment work**

- Read the assigned current source and record version, effective date, definitions, conditions, exceptions, penalty, reviewability, and related mechanics without copying protected wording.
- Write a 500–750 word graduate memorandum on chronological reconstruction of multi-event collegiate plays with a source map and a clearly labeled RefZone instructional-analysis section.
- Create an original four-example comparison: supported, unsupported, borderline, and disciplined no-action.
- Tag two film or simulation clips at normal speed, identify angle limitations, and defend the ruling and mechanics with current authority.
- Record a three-minute oral defense and a ten-second floor, coach, table, crew, evaluator, or supervisor communication.
- Submit one measurable applied-performance goal and the evidence that will verify improvement.
