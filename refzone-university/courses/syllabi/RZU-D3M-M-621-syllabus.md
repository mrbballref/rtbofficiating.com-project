# RZU-D3M-M 621 — Advanced Applied NCAA Division III Men’s Officiating Practicum

**Program:** NCAA Division III Men’s Basketball Officiating Master’s-Level Advanced Officiating Program  
**Academic period:** Year 2 · Term 4  
**Term theme:** Graduate Practicum, Capstone, Comprehensive Examination, and Oral Defense  
**Learning volume:** 3 RefZone graduate credit-equivalent units · 45 engagement hours  
**Prerequisite:** RZU-D3M-M 612  
**Credential status:** Professional graduate degree-level pathway; not an accredited degree or transferable graduate credit  
**Rules basis:** Current NCAA men’s playing rules for the 2025-26 and 2026-27 cycle, approved rulings, interpretations, replay and challenge guidance, authorized mechanics, and verified NCAA Division III men’s basketball modifications and administrative procedures  

A graduate-level NCAA Division III men’s basketball officiating course in advanced applied ncaa division iii men’s officiating practicum, integrating current NCAA men’s playing rules, approved rulings, NCAA Division III conference and championship procedures, advanced mechanics, normal-speed film analysis, written rules work, practical performance, and oral defense.

## Graduate learning outcomes

- Analyze advanced applied ncaa division iii men’s officiating practicum with current NCAA men’s playing-rules authority and NCAA Division III administrative authority.
- Resolve advanced legal-status, mechanics, administration, and communication problems without cross-ruleset contamination.
- Defend conclusions through original written analysis, normal-speed film, case-play work, and oral examination.
- Translate graduate study into measurable crew, evaluator, leadership, or game-performance behavior.

## Fifteen-week sequence

- **Week 1:** Practicum Orientation, Safety, Consent, and Evidence Plan
- **Week 2:** Pre-Assignment Source, Procedure, and Conflict Verification
- **Week 3:** Crew Contact, Travel, Arrival, and Site Readiness
- **Week 4:** Interactive Pregame and Crew Coverage Plan
- **Week 5:** Warm-Up Observation, Table Meeting, and Game Administration
- **Week 6:** First-Half Rules, Mechanics, and Judgment Performance
- **Week 7:** Halftime Crew Review and Adjustment
- **Week 8:** Second-Half Consistency, Physicality, and Game Management
- **Week 9:** End-of-Game Clock, Shot Clock, Timeout, and Challenge Readiness
- **Week 10:** Crew-Chief or Partner Leadership Assignment
- **Week 11:** Postgame Review and Immediate Evidence Capture
- **Week 12:** Incident Report or Atypical-Play Documentation
- **Week 13:** Complete-Game Film Self-Evaluation
- **Week 14:** Evaluator Conference, Remediation, and Re-Demonstration
- **Week 15:** Advanced Practicum Final Performance Defense

## Required graduate submissions

- Current-source graduate memorandum
- Original legal, illegal, borderline, and correct no-call or no-correction case set
- Normal-speed film analysis with angle limitations
- Mechanics, replay, communication, evaluation, or leadership artifact appropriate to the course
- Applied performance goal and verification evidence
- Final oral defense and remediation record

## Grading

- Graduate source memoranda and case analysis: 20%
- Film, mechanics, replay, or communication laboratories: 20%
- Applied performance and professional artifacts: 25%
- Midterm and comprehensive examination: 20%
- Final portfolio and oral defense: 15%

## Completion standard

Minimum 85 percent overall, completion of every mandatory competency gate, and successful oral defense or re-demonstration.

### 1. Practicum Orientation, Safety, Consent, and Evidence Plan

This graduate lesson develops practicum orientation, safety, consent, and evidence plan within Advanced Applied NCAA Division III Men’s Officiating Practicum. The learner integrates current NCAA men’s playing rules, approved rulings, applicable NCAA Division III conference and championship procedures, advanced mechanics, normal-speed film evidence, complete administration, professional communication, and measurable applied performance.

**Objectives**

- Analyze practicum orientation, safety, consent, and evidence plan using current NCAA men’s playing-rules authority and applicable NCAA Division III administrative authority.
- Identify operative legal status, game status, crew responsibility, and the exact evidence required for a defensible decision.
- Distinguish supported, unsupported, borderline, and disciplined no-action outcomes.
- Apply the complete penalty, clock, shot-clock, timeout, challenge, possession, reporting, and resumption sequence when applicable.
- Defend the conclusion, mechanics, communication, and source choice through original writing, film review, applied evidence, and oral examination.
- Translate the lesson into a measurable next-game, crew-leadership, evaluation, or instructional behavior.

**Floor and evidence checklist**

- Pre-assignment verification
- Crew preparation
- Court and table readiness
- Rules and mechanics performance
- Judgment and consistency
- Communication and leadership
- Postgame evidence
- Remediation and re-demonstration

**Decision sequence**

- Verify eligibility, safety, consent, and source readiness.
- Complete pre-assignment and crew preparation.
- Perform rules, mechanics, administration, and communication duties.
- Capture complete-game evidence without interfering with the contest.
- Conduct immediate and film-based self-evaluation.
- Receive anchored evaluator feedback.
- Complete remediation and re-demonstration.
- Defend the final performance portfolio.

**Original graduate scenario**

- Setup: The candidate completes “Practicum Orientation, Safety, Consent, and Evidence Plan” in a supervised game or approved high-fidelity simulation. The evidence includes pregame preparation, complete-game performance, film, and evaluator feedback.
- Conclusion: Meet safety and source gates, perform assigned responsibilities, preserve evidence, complete self-evaluation, receive anchored feedback, remediate material gaps, re-demonstrate competency, and defend the final portfolio.
- Communication: This practicum grade reflects verified preparation, observable game performance, film evidence, remediation, and re-demonstration—not the number of assignments worked.

**Study and assessment work**

- Read the assigned current source and record version, effective date, definitions, conditions, exceptions, penalty, reviewability, and related mechanics without copying protected wording.
- Write a 500–750 word graduate memorandum on practicum orientation, safety, consent, and evidence plan with a source map and a clearly labeled RefZone instructional-analysis section.
- Create an original four-example comparison: supported, unsupported, borderline, and disciplined no-action.
- Tag two film or simulation clips at normal speed, identify angle limitations, and defend the ruling and mechanics with current authority.
- Record a three-minute oral defense and a ten-second floor, coach, table, crew, evaluator, or supervisor communication.
- Submit one measurable applied-performance goal and the evidence that will verify improvement.
