## Course Description

A three-unit doctoral course in the NCAA Division III Men’s PhD-Level Research, Leadership, and Elite Officiating pathway focused on comparative rules systems and policy analysis. The course integrates current NCAA men’s playing-rules authority and NCAA Division III administrative authority, doctoral scholarship, original research or policy work, ethics, equity, professional application, peer review, and oral defense.

## Learning Outcomes

- Critically synthesize the doctoral domain of comparative rules systems and policy analysis using current NCAA men’s playing-rules authority and NCAA Division III administrative authority and validated research evidence.
- Design and defend an appropriate research, policy, analytic, leadership, curriculum, technology, or evaluation method.
- Evaluate alternative explanations, uncertainty, ethics, equity, privacy, copyright, governance, and implementation risk.
- Produce original doctoral writing, evidence visualizations, peer review, revision, and oral defense.
- Translate findings into a responsible professional or institutional contribution.
- Maintain complete source versioning, research records, human-review gates, and academic integrity.

## Lesson Sequence

1. Comparative Rules Method Without Ruleset Contamination
2. NCAA Men’s Rule Architecture and Policy Objectives
3. Comparing Definitions Across Governing Bodies
4. Comparing Timing, Shot-Clock, and Possession Systems
5. Comparing Foul Classifications and Penalty Structures
6. Comparing Contact Philosophies and Freedom of Movement
7. Comparing Replay, Review, and Challenge Systems
8. Comparing Crew Designations, Signals, and Mechanics
9. Comparing Administrative and Bench-Conduct Rules
10. Competition Modifications and Local Policy Layers
11. Policy Transfer Risks and Unintended Consequences
12. Building a Rules-Difference Database
13. Writing a Comparative Policy Brief
14. Stakeholder Impact and Implementation Analysis
15. Comparative Rules Colloquium and Defense

## Source and Copyright Controls

- Official NCAA men’s and NCAA Division III publications and current official updates are reference authorities; student-facing instruction is original RefZone University language.
- No protected rulebook text, casebook language, mechanics diagrams, game footage, logos, or identifiable participant data may be reproduced without authorization.
- Research literature, legal and policy sources, technology claims, and institutional procedures require source-by-source validation.
- Any uncertainty, source conflict, inaccessible authority, ethics concern, or jurisdiction-specific requirement remains blocked for human review.
