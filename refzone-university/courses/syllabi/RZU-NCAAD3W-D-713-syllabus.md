**Program:** NCAA Division III Women’s Basketball Officiating PhD-Level Research, Leadership, and Elite Officiating Pathway  
**Academic placement:** Year 1, Term 2  
**Units:** 3 RefZone doctoral credit-equivalent units  
**Engagement:** 45 hours  
**Prerequisite:** Successful completion of RZU-NCAAD3W-D 711 and all applicable doctoral competency gates  
**Credential status:** Professional doctoral degree-level pathway; not an accredited or transferable academic degree  
**Publication status:** Complete instructional production draft; required human review remains pending  

## Course Description

A three-unit doctoral course in the NCAA Division III Women’s PhD-Level Research, Leadership, and Elite Officiating pathway focused on performance analytics and evidence-based evaluation. The course integrates current NCAA women’s playing-rules authority and NCAA Division III administrative authority, doctoral scholarship, original research or policy work, ethics, equity, professional application, peer review, and oral defense.

## Learning Outcomes

- Critically synthesize the doctoral domain of performance analytics and evidence-based evaluation using current NCAA women’s playing-rules authority and NCAA Division III administrative authority and validated research evidence.
- Design and defend an appropriate research, policy, analytic, leadership, curriculum, technology, or evaluation method.
- Evaluate alternative explanations, uncertainty, ethics, equity, privacy, copyright, governance, and implementation risk.
- Produce original doctoral writing, evidence visualizations, peer review, revision, and oral defense.
- Translate findings into a responsible professional or institutional contribution.
- Maintain complete source versioning, research records, human-review gates, and academic integrity.

## Lesson Sequence

1. What Should Officiating Analytics Measure?
2. Constructs, Indicators, Metrics, and Observable Behavior
3. Call Accuracy, Process Quality, and Opportunity Sets
4. No-Call Evaluation and Denominator Problems
5. Mechanics, Positioning, and Coverage Metrics
6. Clock, Administration, and Communication Metrics
7. Reliability, Validity, and Measurement Error
8. Inter-Rater Agreement and Evaluator Calibration
9. Context, Game Difficulty, and Risk Adjustment
10. Dashboards, Data Visualization, and Responsible Interpretation
11. Small Samples, Regression to the Mean, and Overclaiming
12. Combining Film, Observer, Self-Report, and Performance Data
13. Designing an Anchored Evaluation Instrument
14. Evidence-Based Development Plan and Feedback Conference
15. Performance Analytics Defense

## Source and Copyright Controls

- Official NCAA women’s and NCAA Division III publications and current official updates are reference authorities; student-facing instruction is original RefZone University language.
- No protected rulebook text, casebook language, mechanics diagrams, game footage, logos, or identifiable participant data may be reproduced without authorization.
- Research literature, legal and policy sources, technology claims, and institutional procedures require source-by-source validation.
- Any uncertainty, source conflict, inaccessible authority, ethics concern, or jurisdiction-specific requirement remains blocked for human review.
