## Course Description

A three-unit doctoral course in the NCAA Division II Women’s PhD-Level Research, Leadership, and Elite Officiating pathway focused on decision science and visual cognition. The course integrates current NCAA women’s playing-rules authority and NCAA Division II administrative authority, doctoral scholarship, original research or policy work, ethics, equity, professional application, peer review, and oral defense.

## Learning Outcomes

- Critically synthesize the doctoral domain of decision science and visual cognition using current NCAA women’s playing-rules authority and NCAA Division II administrative authority and validated research evidence.
- Design and defend an appropriate research, policy, analytic, leadership, curriculum, technology, or evaluation method.
- Evaluate alternative explanations, uncertainty, ethics, equity, privacy, copyright, governance, and implementation risk.
- Produce original doctoral writing, evidence visualizations, peer review, revision, and oral defense.
- Translate findings into a responsible professional or institutional contribution.
- Maintain complete source versioning, research records, human-review gates, and academic integrity.

## Lesson Sequence

1. Decision Science for Real-Time Officiating
2. Perception, Interpretation, Decision, and Action
3. Visual Acuity, Contrast, Motion, and Court Geometry
4. Saccades, Fixations, Gaze Anchors, and Visual Search
5. Angle, Distance, Occlusion, and Straight-Line Risk
6. Temporal Resolution and Whistle Timing
7. Legal-Status Recognition Before Contact
8. Signal Detection: Hits, Misses, False Alarms, and Correct Rejections
9. Confidence, Uncertainty, and Decision Thresholds
10. Outcome Bias and the Falling-Player Effect
11. Normal Speed, Slow Motion, and Fact Reconstruction
12. Decision Trees and Evidence Requirements
13. Designing a Visual-Cognition Film Laboratory
14. Measuring Decision Process Rather Than Outcome Alone
15. Doctoral Decision-Science Examination

- Official NCAA women’s and NCAA Division II publications and current official updates are reference authorities; student-facing instruction is original RefZone University language.
