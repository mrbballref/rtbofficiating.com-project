# RZU-D3M-M 512 — Advanced Three-Person Mechanics and Coverage Extensions

**Program:** NCAA Division III Men’s Basketball Officiating Master’s-Level Advanced Officiating Program  
**Academic period:** Year 1 · Term 2  
**Term theme:** Advanced Mechanics, Timing, Replay, and Game Administration  
**Learning volume:** 3 RefZone graduate credit-equivalent units · 45 engagement hours  
**Prerequisite:** RZU-D3M-M 511  
**Credential status:** Professional graduate degree-level pathway; not an accredited degree or transferable graduate credit  
**Rules basis:** Current NCAA men’s playing rules for the 2025-26 and 2026-27 cycle, approved rulings, interpretations, replay and challenge guidance, authorized mechanics, and verified NCAA Division III men’s basketball modifications and administrative procedures  

A graduate-level NCAA Division III men’s basketball officiating course in advanced three-person mechanics and coverage extensions, integrating current NCAA men’s playing rules, approved rulings, NCAA Division III conference and championship procedures, advanced mechanics, normal-speed film analysis, written rules work, practical performance, and oral defense.

## Graduate learning outcomes

- Analyze advanced three-person mechanics and coverage extensions with current NCAA men’s playing-rules authority and NCAA Division III administrative authority.
- Resolve advanced legal-status, mechanics, administration, and communication problems without cross-ruleset contamination.
- Defend conclusions through original written analysis, normal-speed film, case-play work, and oral examination.
- Translate graduate study into measurable crew, evaluator, leadership, or game-performance behavior.

## Fifteen-week sequence

- **Week 1:** Graduate Three-Person Mechanics Philosophy and Crew Geometry
- **Week 2:** Lead Positioning, Baseline Adjustments, and Paint Matchups
- **Week 3:** Center Positioning, Weak-Side Awareness, and Drive Coverage
- **Week 4:** Trail Positioning, Perimeter Matchups, Counts, and Clock Responsibilities
- **Week 5:** Primary Coverage, Secondary Coverage, and Extended Coverage
- **Week 6:** Lead-Initiated Rotations and Crew Response
- **Week 7:** Late, Aborted, Emergency, and Missed Rotations
- **Week 8:** Drives, Secondary Defenders, and Restricted-Area Coverage
- **Week 9:** Post Play, Screening, and Off-Ball Coverage Distribution
- **Week 10:** Rebounding, Goaltending, Basket Interference, and Help Responsibilities
- **Week 11:** Transition, Press, Long Passes, and Fast-Break Coverage
- **Week 12:** Throw-Ins, Free Throws, Timeouts, and Intermissions
- **Week 13:** Double Whistles, Help Calls, and Factual Crew Conferences
- **Week 14:** End-of-Period Mechanics and Last-Shot Responsibilities
- **Week 15:** Advanced Three-Person Mechanics Film Practical

## Required graduate submissions

- Current-source graduate memorandum
- Original legal, illegal, borderline, and correct no-call or no-correction case set
- Normal-speed film analysis with angle limitations
- Mechanics, replay, communication, evaluation, or leadership artifact appropriate to the course
- Applied performance goal and verification evidence
- Final oral defense and remediation record

## Grading

- Graduate source memoranda and case analysis: 20%
- Film, mechanics, replay, or communication laboratories: 20%
- Applied performance and professional artifacts: 25%
- Midterm and comprehensive examination: 20%
- Final portfolio and oral defense: 15%

## Completion standard

Minimum 85 percent overall, completion of every mandatory competency gate, and successful oral defense or re-demonstration.

### 1. Graduate Three-Person Mechanics Philosophy and Crew Geometry

This graduate lesson develops graduate three-person mechanics philosophy and crew geometry within Advanced Three-Person Mechanics and Coverage Extensions. The learner integrates current NCAA men’s playing rules, approved rulings, applicable NCAA Division III conference and championship procedures, advanced mechanics, normal-speed film evidence, complete administration, professional communication, and measurable applied performance.

**Objectives**

- Analyze graduate three-person mechanics philosophy and crew geometry using current NCAA men’s playing-rules authority and applicable NCAA Division III administrative authority.
- Identify operative legal status, game status, crew responsibility, and the exact evidence required for a defensible decision.
- Distinguish supported, unsupported, borderline, and disciplined no-action outcomes.
- Apply the complete penalty, clock, shot-clock, timeout, challenge, possession, reporting, and resumption sequence when applicable.
- Defend the conclusion, mechanics, communication, and source choice through original writing, film review, applied evidence, and oral examination.
- Translate the lesson into a measurable next-game, crew-leadership, evaluation, or instructional behavior.

**Floor and evidence checklist**

- Crew starting positions
- Primary matchup
- Open or closed angle
- Distance from play
- Rotation status
- Secondary-help evidence
- Clock and shot-clock responsibilities
- Post-call reporting and new position

**Decision sequence**

- Identify primary coverage and the matchup before movement.
- Move to maintain an open angle and useful distance.
- Recognize rotation, transition, or position-adjustment triggers.
- Stay with primary action through its consequence.
- Use secondary help only with complete factual evidence.
- Resolve double whistles through crew protocol.
- Report accurately and assume the correct new position.
- Review positioning and ownership on film.

**Original graduate scenario**

- Setup: During “Graduate Three-Person Mechanics Philosophy and Crew Geometry,” the crew’s first positions create a closed angle. A late movement opens the play for one official while tempting another to reach outside primary coverage.
- Conclusion: Identify the correct position adjustment, primary and secondary responsibilities, rotation status, whistle ownership, reporting sequence, and new position. Use film to compare the straight-lined and open-angle views.
- Communication: The calling official had the open primary look. The crew confirmed the factual information, administered the result, and moved to the correct ensuing positions.

**Study and assessment work**

- Read the assigned current source and record version, effective date, definitions, conditions, exceptions, penalty, reviewability, and related mechanics without copying protected wording.
- Write a 500–750 word graduate memorandum on graduate three-person mechanics philosophy and crew geometry with a source map and a clearly labeled RefZone instructional-analysis section.
- Create an original four-example comparison: supported, unsupported, borderline, and disciplined no-action.
- Tag two film or simulation clips at normal speed, identify angle limitations, and defend the ruling and mechanics with current authority.
- Record a three-minute oral defense and a ten-second floor, coach, table, crew, evaluator, or supervisor communication.
- Submit one measurable applied-performance goal and the evidence that will verify improvement.
