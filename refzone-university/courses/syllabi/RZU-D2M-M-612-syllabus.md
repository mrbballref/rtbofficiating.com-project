# RZU-D2M-M 612 — Graduate Film Analysis and Performance Analytics

**Program:** NCAA Division II Men’s Basketball Officiating Master’s-Level Advanced Officiating Program  
**Academic period:** Year 2 · Term 3  
**Term theme:** Leadership, Evaluation, Communication, and Film Analytics  
**Learning volume:** 3 RefZone graduate credit-equivalent units · 45 engagement hours  
**Prerequisite:** RZU-D2M-M 611  
**Credential status:** Professional graduate degree-level pathway; not an accredited degree or transferable graduate credit  
**Rules basis:** Current NCAA men’s playing rules for the 2025-26 and 2026-27 cycle, approved rulings, interpretations, replay and challenge guidance, authorized mechanics, and verified NCAA Division II men’s basketball modifications and administrative procedures  

A graduate-level NCAA Division II men’s basketball officiating course in graduate film analysis and performance analytics, integrating current NCAA men’s playing rules, approved rulings, NCAA Division II conference and championship procedures, advanced mechanics, normal-speed film analysis, written rules work, practical performance, and oral defense.

## Graduate learning outcomes

- Analyze graduate film analysis and performance analytics with current NCAA men’s playing-rules authority and NCAA Division II administrative authority.
- Resolve advanced legal-status, mechanics, administration, and communication problems without cross-ruleset contamination.
- Defend conclusions through original written analysis, normal-speed film, case-play work, and oral examination.
- Translate graduate study into measurable crew, evaluator, leadership, or game-performance behavior.

## Fifteen-week sequence

- **Week 1:** Graduate Film-Study Method: Normal Speed First and Last
- **Week 2:** Tagging Matchup, Legal Status, Coverage, and Decisive Evidence
- **Week 3:** Primary and Secondary Angle Comparison
- **Week 4:** Straight-Line Risk and Open-Angle Analysis
- **Week 5:** Patient Whistle, Late Whistle, and Outcome Bias
- **Week 6:** Call, No-Call, and No-Correction Accuracy Coding
- **Week 7:** Mechanics, Positioning, and Rotation Analytics
- **Week 8:** Clock, Shot Clock, Replay, and Administration Analytics
- **Week 9:** Communication and Reporting Performance Review
- **Week 10:** Self-Scouting Trends and Root-Cause Analysis
- **Week 11:** Building a Meaningful Performance Dashboard
- **Week 12:** Sample Size, Context, and Misleading Metrics
- **Week 13:** Turning Film Evidence Into a Practice Plan
- **Week 14:** Complete-Game Film Defense
- **Week 15:** Graduate Performance-Analytics Portfolio

## Required graduate submissions

- Current-source graduate memorandum
- Original legal, illegal, borderline, and correct no-call or no-correction case set
- Normal-speed film analysis with angle limitations
- Mechanics, replay, communication, evaluation, or leadership artifact appropriate to the course
- Applied performance goal and verification evidence
- Final oral defense and remediation record

## Grading

- Graduate source memoranda and case analysis: 20%
- Film, mechanics, replay, or communication laboratories: 20%
- Applied performance and professional artifacts: 25%
- Midterm and comprehensive examination: 20%
- Final portfolio and oral defense: 15%

## Completion standard

Minimum 85 percent overall, completion of every mandatory competency gate, and successful oral defense or re-demonstration.

- Defend the conclusion, mechanics, communication, and source choice through original writing, film review, applied evidence, and oral examination.

**Floor and evidence checklist**

- Normal-speed action
- Crew position
- Primary matchup
- Legal status
- Decisive evidence
- Ruling and administration
- Alternate-angle limitations
- Next-game application

**Decision sequence**

- Watch normal speed without stopping.
- Identify crew position, primary matchup, and legal status.
- Locate decisive evidence and the ruling on the floor.
- Apply current rules and mechanics.
- Review alternate angles and slow motion only to identify facts.
- Return to normal speed to assess real-time judgment.
- Code the result and root cause consistently.
- Create a measurable next-game practice target.
