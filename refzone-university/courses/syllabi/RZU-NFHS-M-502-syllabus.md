# RZU-NFHS-M 502 — Complex Case Plays and Penalty Sequencing

**Program:** NFHS Basketball Officiating Master’s-Level Advanced Officiating Program  
**Academic period:** Year 1 · Term 1  
**Term theme:** Advanced Rules Interpretation, Penalty Sequencing, and Contact Judgment  
**Learning volume:** 3 RefZone graduate credit-equivalent units · 45 engagement hours  
**Prerequisite:** Successful completion of RZU-NFHS-M 501 and all applicable graduate competency gates  
**Credential status:** Professional graduate degree-level pathway; not represented as an accredited degree or transferable graduate credit  
**Publication status:** Complete graduate instructional draft pending qualified rules, mechanics, curriculum, copyright, accessibility, assessment, video, and program review  

A three-unit graduate course in the NFHS Master’s-Level Advanced Officiating pathway focused on complex case plays and penalty sequencing. The course combines current-source seminars, original complex cases, advanced film and mechanics analysis, professional communication, applied performance, and oral defense.

## Graduate learning outcomes

- Analyze and defend the advanced competency domain of complex case plays and penalty sequencing using current NFHS authority.
- Resolve complex fact patterns through legal status, chronology, definitions, exceptions, mechanics ownership, and complete administration.
- Distinguish legal, illegal, borderline, and correct no-call or no-correction outcomes under normal-speed conditions.
- Produce graduate-level written analysis, film evidence, professional communication, and oral defense.
- Demonstrate advanced leadership, evaluation, or applied officiating behavior appropriate to the course domain.
- Identify source limitations, local-policy requirements, copyright restrictions, and human-review gates.

## Fifteen-week graduate sequence

- **Week 1:** Chronological Reconstruction of Complex Plays
- **Week 2:** Ball Status, Player Control, and Team Control in Multi-Event Plays
- **Week 3:** Multiple Fouls and Independent Penalties
- **Week 4:** Double and Simultaneous Fouls
- **Week 5:** False Double Fouls and Intervening Live-Ball Action
- **Week 6:** Technical Fouls During the Same Dead-Ball Period
- **Week 7:** Intentional and Flagrant Acts Within a Penalty Sequence · midterm rules memorandum workshop
- **Week 8:** Fighting, Bench Participation, and Disqualification Sequencing · written and oral midterm examination
- **Week 9:** Correctable Errors and Time-Limited Correction Windows
- **Week 10:** Wrong Shooter, Wrong Basket, and Incorrect Free-Throw Administration
- **Week 11:** Inadvertent Whistles and Point-of-Interruption Analysis
- **Week 12:** Book, Score, Clock, and Table Discrepancies
- **Week 13:** End-of-Period and Overtime Multi-Event Cases
- **Week 14:** Rare-Play Crew Conference and Final Ruling Presentation · final project presentation
- **Week 15:** Complex Case-Play Written and Oral Examination · oral defense and remediation conference

### 1. Chronological Reconstruction of Complex Plays

This graduate lesson develops chronological reconstruction of complex plays within Complex Case Plays and Penalty Sequencing. The learner must integrate current source authority, advanced visual evidence, ruleset-specific mechanics, complete administration, professional communication, film defense, and measurable applied performance.

**Graduate evidence required**

- Read the assigned current source and record version, effective date, definitions, conditions, exceptions, penalty, and related mechanics without copying protected wording.
- Write a 500–750 word graduate memorandum on chronological reconstruction of complex plays with a source map and a clearly labeled RefZone instructional analysis section.
- Create an original four-play comparison: legal, illegal, borderline, and correct no-call or no-correction.
- Tag two film clips at normal speed, identify angle limitations, and defend the ruling and mechanics with current authority.
- Record a three-minute oral defense and a ten-second game-floor communication.
