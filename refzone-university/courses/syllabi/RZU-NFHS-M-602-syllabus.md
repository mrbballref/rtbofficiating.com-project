# RZU-NFHS-M 602 — Crew-Chief Leadership and Crew Performance

**Program:** NFHS Basketball Officiating Master’s-Level Advanced Officiating Program  
**Academic period:** Year 2 · Term 3  
**Term theme:** Game Management, Crew-Chief Leadership, Evaluation, and Instruction  
**Learning volume:** 3 RefZone graduate credit-equivalent units · 45 engagement hours  
**Prerequisite:** Successful completion of RZU-NFHS-M 601 and all applicable graduate competency gates  
**Credential status:** Professional graduate degree-level pathway; not represented as an accredited degree or transferable graduate credit  
**Publication status:** Complete graduate instructional draft pending qualified rules, mechanics, curriculum, copyright, accessibility, assessment, video, and program review  

A three-unit graduate course in the NFHS Master’s-Level Advanced Officiating pathway focused on crew-chief leadership and crew performance. The course combines current-source seminars, original complex cases, advanced film and mechanics analysis, professional communication, applied performance, and oral defense.

## Graduate learning outcomes

- Analyze and defend the advanced competency domain of crew-chief leadership and crew performance using current NFHS authority.
- Resolve complex fact patterns through legal status, chronology, definitions, exceptions, mechanics ownership, and complete administration.
- Distinguish legal, illegal, borderline, and correct no-call or no-correction outcomes under normal-speed conditions.
- Produce graduate-level written analysis, film evidence, professional communication, and oral defense.
- Demonstrate advanced leadership, evaluation, or applied officiating behavior appropriate to the course domain.
- Identify source limitations, local-policy requirements, copyright restrictions, and human-review gates.

## Fifteen-week graduate sequence

- **Week 1:** Crew Chief as Facilitator, Not Dominator
- **Week 2:** Assignment Acceptance, Conflict Review, and Partner Contact
- **Week 3:** Travel, Arrival, Site Verification, and Contingency Planning
- **Week 4:** Building an Interactive, Ruleset-Specific Pregame
- **Week 5:** Court, Equipment, Table, Security, and Medical Verification
- **Week 6:** Defining Primary Coverage, Help Philosophy, and Double Whistles
- **Week 7:** Leading During Timing, Scoring, and Administrative Errors · midterm rules memorandum workshop
- **Week 8:** Leading Flagrant, Fighting, and Safety Conferences · written and oral midterm examination
- **Week 9:** Supporting Partners and Correcting Factual Errors
- **Week 10:** Halftime Adjustments Without Over-Officiating
- **Week 11:** Postgame Review, Film Priorities, and Confidentiality
- **Week 12:** Supervisor and Assignor Communication
- **Week 13:** Feedback, Accountability, and Partner Development
- **Week 14:** Crew Performance Plan and Leadership Practicum · final project presentation
- **Week 15:** Crew-Chief Oral Board and Leadership Defense · oral defense and remediation conference

## Lesson requirements

### 1. Crew Chief as Facilitator, Not Dominator

This graduate lesson develops crew chief as facilitator, not dominator within Crew-Chief Leadership and Crew Performance. The learner must integrate current source authority, advanced visual evidence, ruleset-specific mechanics, complete administration, professional communication, film defense, and measurable applied performance.

**Graduate evidence required**

- Read the assigned current source and record version, effective date, definitions, conditions, exceptions, penalty, and related mechanics without copying protected wording.
- Write a 500–750 word graduate memorandum on crew chief as facilitator, not dominator with a source map and a clearly labeled RefZone instructional analysis section.
- Create an original four-play comparison: legal, illegal, borderline, and correct no-call or no-correction.
- Tag two film clips at normal speed, identify angle limitations, and defend the ruling and mechanics with current authority.
- Record a three-minute oral defense and a ten-second game-floor communication.
- Submit one measurable applied-performance goal and the evidence that will verify improvement.

## Completion requirements

- Earn at least 85 percent for the graduate course.
- Pass every mandatory rules, penalty, safety, ethics, source-control, and professional-conduct competency.
- Complete all required written, case, film, communication, leadership, evaluation, or practicum submissions.
- Correct any superseded-source, ruleset-transfer, mechanics, or administration error identified during review.
- Pass the final graduate project and oral defense or complete assigned remediation and re-demonstration.

## Source, copyright, and academic-integrity standard

Official NFHS publications and current state, league, tournament, and assigning-authority policies control accuracy. RefZone University course text, scenarios, assessments, diagrams, dialogue, film prompts, and video scripts are original. Protected publication text, case language, mechanics diagrams, footage, logos, and likenesses are not reproduced without authorization. Any source conflict, missing local policy, or disputed ruling remains draft until qualified human review.
